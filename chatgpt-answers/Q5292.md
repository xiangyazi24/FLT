# 8. Consequence for the N49 proof

The p=3 result is useful but not terminal:

1. Every affine residue class with b\ne0 is excluded.

1. Several boundary cells are also excluded.

1. The three projective cells (0,0), (0,\infty), and (\infty,\infty) remain.

1. Even after imposing a\ne0, the (\infty,\infty) cell remains.

There is also a logical problem with discarding the a=0 row. A rational number b\ne0 may have v_3(b)>0; after choosing primitive numerator-denominator coordinates, its numerator then reduces to a=0 modulo 3. Hence the characteristic-zero hypothesis b\ne0 does not justify the residue-field condition a\ne0.

Finally, the surviving cells are conceptually unavoidable for a one-prime local obstruction. The split Tate curve over \mathbf Q_3 with parameter q=3^{49} has the class of 3 as a rational point of exact order 49. Putting that marked point into Tate normal form gives a \mathbf Q_3 solution with H_{49}=0, so denominator clearing must leave at least one projective zero modulo 3.
