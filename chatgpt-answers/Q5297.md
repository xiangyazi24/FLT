# 9. Recommended Lean decomposition

A practical formalization can avoid both a generic modular-curve library and the 3526-term polynomial.

1. Tate-to-short bridge. Define S_0 and P_s by the formulas in §2.1 and verify the curve equation by ring.

1. Fixed Velu7. Implement only the order-7 quotient formulas, using the three \pm-pairs. Prove the map equations by field_simp; ring.

1. Two quotient steps. Construct S_1,Q,S_2 and prove Q has exact order 7.

1. Invariant extraction. Define the homogeneous (-\widetilde C_0:\widetilde C_1) formulas for u,v.

1. Target coordinates. Define (t:1)=(N:D), then x_0,y_0, with a finite alternate-chart split at common zeros.

1. Cuspidality. Use t^{24}\Delta_2=7^{24}\Delta_0 to prove the target is not either rational cusp.

# Direct answers

1. Explicit map: yes—the boxed composition in §5 is an explicit rational function of b,c. It is best retained in factored two-Vélu form; a fully expanded pair would be impractically large.

1. Rational cusps: \infty\mapsto O and 0\mapsto(2,-1).

1. Rank zero in Lean: yes, by the fixed 2-isogeny descent in §7, provided one supplies a bespoke global height/Mordell–Weil bridge. The finite certificates are only computations modulo 16 and 49.

1. Exact order maps away from the cusps: yes. Under nonsingularity, F_{49}=0, and F_7\ne0, the discriminant ratio forces t\ne0,\infty, so the image is neither O nor (2,-1).
