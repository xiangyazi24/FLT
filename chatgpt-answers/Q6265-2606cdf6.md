## What the present F₂-length results prove — and what they do not

Suppose the current file has a localized affine quotient Q and has proved

```javascript

Module.length (ZMod 2) Q = 1   -- Z contribution
```

These are valuable finite-algebra facts, but three additional identifications are needed.

1. Principal open/localized affine ring → point local ring. A section ring on a basic open is an Away localization. A stalk is an AtPrime localization. They are not definitionally or mathematically the same object unless one proves the point-local comparison.

1. Hyperplane quotient → stalk quotient. One must prove that the current Q is the quotient of the local ring at the named point by the local hyperplane equation, not merely a ring with the same finite F₂ dimension.

1. F₂ length → local-ring length. One must prove the residue-field factor is one.

The second and third steps are the smallest useful next formal atoms.

### 5. Surjective quotient maps do not change length when one passes to the quotient ring as scalar ring

and

```javascript

    Module.length A (A ⧸ I) =
      Module.length (A ⧸ I) (A ⧸ I) := by
```

## Recommended generic bridge lemmas

I would put the following in a small local helper file imported by the N25 hyperplane file. They isolate all generic length bookkeeping from the project geometry.

```javascript
import Mathlib.RingTheory.Ideal.Quotient.Operations
import Mathlib.RingTheory.Length
import Mathlib.RingTheory.LocalRing.Length
import Mathlib.RingTheory.LocalRing.Quotient
import Mathlib.RingTheory.OrderOfVanishing.Basic
import Mathlib.AlgebraicGeometry.StructureSheaf

lemma length_quotient_eq_length_self
    {A : Type*} [CommRing A] (I : Ideal A) :
    Module.length A (A ⧸ I) =
      Module.length (A ⧸ I) (A ⧸ I) := by
  apply Module.length_eq_of_surjective
  simpa only [Ideal.Quotient.algebraMap_eq] using
    (Ideal.Quotient.mk_surjective (I := I))

lemma self_length_eq_base_length_of_residue_length_one
    {k B : Type*}
    [Field k] [CommRing B] [Nontrivial B]
    [Algebra k B] [IsLocalRing B]
    (hκ :
      Module.length (IsLocalRing.ResidueField k)
        (IsLocalRing.ResidueField B) = 1) :
    Module.length B B = Module.length k B := by
  have h := IsLocalRing.length_restrictScalars
    (A := k) (B := B) (M := B)
  rw [hκ, mul_one] at h
  exact h.symm

lemma local_quotient_length_eq_base_length_of_residue_length_one
    {k A : Type*}
    [Field k] [CommRing A] [IsLocalRing A]
    [Algebra k A]
    (I : Ideal A) (hI : I ≠ ⊤)
    (hκ :
      Module.length (IsLocalRing.ResidueField k)
        (IsLocalRing.ResidueField (A ⧸ I)) = 1) :
    Module.length A (A ⧸ I) =
      Module.length k (A ⧸ I) := by
  letI : Nontrivial (A ⧸ I) :=
    Ideal.Quotient.nontrivial_iff.mpr hI
  letI : IsLocalRing (A ⧸ I) :=
    IsLocalRing.of_surjective'
      (Ideal.Quotient.mk I)
      Ideal.Quotient.mk_surjective
  calc
    Module.length A (A ⧸ I) =
        Module.length (A ⧸ I) (A ⧸ I) :=
      length_quotient_eq_length_self I
    _ = Module.length k (A ⧸ I) :=
      self_length_eq_base_length_of_residue_length_one hκ
```

The third lemma is the exact formal firewall the current development needs. If A is the point local ring and I = (h), then it says: F₂ length equals local intersection length only after the residue-field-degree-one proof has been supplied.

## How to prove the residue-field factor is one

There are two honest short routes.

### Route A: use the named F₂-rational point

If RationalPointsN25QuotientTwoFullClosedPoints.lean already proves that the point corresponding to p has residue field exactly ZMod 2, use that. The quotient Bp = Ap/(h) has the same residue field as Ap provided h lies in the maximal ideal, which follows from “the hyperplane passes through the point.” Formalize that statement; do not infer it from the final length.

This route is geometrically cleanest because the same closed-point object later appears in the cycle.

### Route B: use an augmentation of the Artin model

and prove

```javascript

RingHom.ker ε = IsLocalRing.maximalIdeal Q
```

Then the first-isomorphism theorem gives an explicit ring equivalence

## Global support and chart coverage: every missing input

The following inputs are still required before one may state 2·[W]+[Z] and 3·[YZ] as global hyperplane sections.

1. Actual affine-chart model. Prove that the ring used by the Artin-local calculation is the coordinate ring of an open subscheme of the projective N25 quotient curve, not merely an auxiliary affine quotient.

1. Restriction of the global section. Prove that global homogeneous W restricts to the affine element used in the W calculation, and global Y+Z restricts to the affine element used in the YZ calculation. This is essential for calling the local quotient a hyperplane section.

1. Named point ↔ prime compatibility. Prove that the projective closed points [W], [Z], [YZ] map to the exact primes used in the AtPrime localizations.

1. Local quotient comparison. Prove (O_{X,p})/(h_p) is the explicit finite algebra whose F₂ length was computed.

1. Localness/nontriviality. Establish the quotient/local Artin ring is nontrivial and local; an Artin-model equivalence is a reasonable route.

1. Residue degree one. Prove each relevant residue field is F₂, or at minimum prove the residue-field length factor in IsLocalRing.length_restrictScalars is 1.

1. Effectivity/Cartier regularity. To say the hyperplane defines an effective Cartier divisor, prove its local equation is a non-zero-divisor wherever needed. On an integral curve this follows from the global section being nonzero at the generic point, but integrality and nonvanishing must themselves be formal inputs. Finite quotient length does not by itself prove the Cartier condition.

1. Support inside the computed chart. Prove that within the chart, W=0 has no closed point other than the W and Z points, and Y+Z=0 has no closed point other than YZ.

1. Chart complement / points at infinity. Prove there is no zero of either hyperplane section on the complement of the chart. The localized affine calculation says nothing about this complement.

1. Closed-point cycle finiteness. Once support is known to be the displayed finite set, package the coefficients into the existing full closed-point grading/finsupp.

1. Global divisor packaging. Only after the previous items should the cycle be named the effective hyperplane divisor, unless the project already has a definition whose hypotheses are exactly those proved.

## Points at infinity: what must actually be shown

I cannot certify from the accessible source that the chosen chart covers either entire hyperplane section. Therefore “no points at infinity” is presently an unproved geometric assertion.

If the affine chart is the standard open D(s) for some homogeneous coordinate/section s, the missing statements are of the form

```plain text
X ∩ V(W) ∩ V(s) = ∅
X ∩ V(Y+Z) ∩ V(s) = ∅.
```

There are three realistic formal routes, in increasing abstraction.

1. Direct complement-chart computation. Cover V(s) by the remaining standard projective charts and simplify the homogeneous equations there. This is usually the shortest Lean proof if there are only a few coordinates.

1. Homogeneous-ideal/saturation calculation. Prove that I_X + (W,s) and I_X + (Y+Z,s) have no relevant homogeneous prime, equivalently that their saturation is irrelevant. This is algebraically robust but may require more projective-geometry infrastructure.

1. Degree argument. If the project already proves the hyperplane-section degree and effectivity, then the local coefficients sum to the total degree and exclude extra support. Do not use this route unless the degree theorem is already formalized; otherwise it is much heavier than checking the complement.

Given the current state described in the question, route 1 is the likely shortest.

The new full degreewise closed-point grading is helpful for stating the support theorem across all closed points, including non-rational ones. It does not by itself prove that the hyperplane does not vanish at a point of higher degree or at infinity. That requires a vanishing/membership theorem for the homogeneous section.

## Effectivity versus an effective closed-point cycle

There are two distinct formal targets and they should not be conflated.

### Target A: effective closed-point intersection cycle

This needs:

- a multiplicity function at closed points, preferably Ring.ord of the local equation;

- the global support/no-infinity theorem.

Then one can prove, in the project’s closed-point grading,

```plain text

cycle(Y + Z) = 3·[YZ].
```

This is the strongest near-term target I recommend.

### Target B: effective Cartier divisor

This additionally needs a global line-bundle section and regularity/non-zero-divisor data. Pinned Mathlib does not expose a ready-made CartierDivisor/WeilDivisor API in this revision, and the newer scheme-level Mathlib/AlgebraicGeometry/OrderOfVanishing.lean is not present at the pin. Therefore trying to force Target B now would create infrastructure work unrelated to the N25 coefficient calculation.

If the project later wants the semantic statement “the hyperplane section is an effective divisor,” first prove Target A plus the regular-section theorem, then add a thin project-level divisor wrapper.
