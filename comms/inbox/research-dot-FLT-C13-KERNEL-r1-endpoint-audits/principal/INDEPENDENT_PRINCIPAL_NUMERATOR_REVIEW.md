## 1. Actual multiplier and both infinity orders

`multiplier_of_centered_double` assumes the displayed actual centered Picard equality for disk pairs P and Q. The centered chart is `pic(P)-pic(B)`, with B the actual basePair. Therefore centeredPic(Q)=2*centeredPic(P) rearranges to

`pic(P)+pic(P)=pic(Q)+pic(B)`.

The proof's two additive calculations have the correct base multiplicity: the intermediate expression includes two copies of pic(B), and the final result retains one base divisor alongside Q.

The Picard quotient is the additive wrapper of the quotient of oriented invertible fractional ideals by the range of principalOriented. Re-expressing the additive class equation as equality of the images of the two raw products uses the actual `classOf` and `mumfordRaw` definitions, not a separate equality assumption.

The exact pinned quotient theorem is

`mk' N x = mk' N y ↔ ∃ a∈N, x*a=y`.

Thus the extracted principal multiplier α has precisely the orientation asserted by the candidate:

`I(P)*I(P)*principal(α)=I(Q)*I(B)`.

It is neither inverted nor silently replaced later. Range membership gives a unit α in the actual function field.

Every disk-pair Mumford representative has nInf=0, and `mumfordRaw` stores the integer nInf-1. Each raw representative therefore has infinity mark -1; both tensor products have mark -2. The second component of the same oriented multiplier equality gives `-2+ordPlus(α)=-2`, so its positive order is zero.

The already reviewed `N13PrincipalBranchBalance.principal_branch_orders_sum` applies to the exact affine four-factor relation extracted above. Its right side is `deg(P)+deg(P)-deg(Q)-deg(B)`. Each actual sextic semi-Mumford polynomial has degree two, so the sum of the positive and negative orders is zero. With positive order already zero, negative order is zero as well. The proof does not assume this second order, omit a degree term, or replace the multiplier while deriving it.

## 2. Product-ideal membership on a single regular numerator

Hence each mapped xClass(uᵢ) belongs to the product Iᵢ*Jᵢ, not merely to one factor. The principal fractional ideal generated by α contains α itself.

Multiplying these three membership facts places

`map(u₀)*map(u₁)*α`

Only after establishing that full product membership does the proof use the fractional-ideal embedding of an integral ideal. The exact `mem_coeIdeal` API returns one element n in this product ideal together with equality of its field image to the given fraction. The final rearrangement uses xClass multiplicativity and the scalar algebra map to produce exactly `map(n)=α*map(xClass(u₀*u₁))`. No cancellation is used in this construction. In particular, no relative primality of the original two factors is assumed; the repeated-P specialization is allowed.
