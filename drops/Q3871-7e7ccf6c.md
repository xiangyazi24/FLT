# Cyclic order 27 route

A rational point `P` of order `27` gives a rational cyclic subgroup `<P>` of order `27`, hence a rational point on `X0(27)`.  This avoids writing down the quotient curve explicitly, although using a concrete Fermat-cubic model still requires proving the modular-curve identification or equivalent rational formulas.

There is no same-curve group-theory contradiction: `3P` has order `9`, `9P` has order `3`, and `9P = 3*(3P)`, so the torsion remains cyclic.

For a degree-3 quotient by `<9P>`, the dual kernel is generally only a rational subgroup of order `3`, not necessarily a rational point of order `3`.  Thus this does not automatically create rational `Z/3 x Z/9` torsion on the quotient.

The Tate-normal-form attack with `P` at the origin is the exact-order system `psi_27(0,0)=0` and `psi_9(0,0) != 0`; this is a model of `X1(27)` and is much harder than the genus-one `X0(27)` Fermat-cubic route.  If instead `3P` is put at the origin, then `F9=0` and the extra condition is the existence of a rational preimage under multiplication by `3`, not another useful `F27` condition.
