# Cyclic order 16: the correct Kubert relation, the missing square, and why the elliptic target does not apply

## Verdict

There are three separate facts.

1. A rational point of exact order 16 gives the usual genus-2 modular curve `X₁(16)`. In Tate normal form the canonical raw Kubert equation is `F₁₆(r,s)=0`, displayed below. This uses only the cyclic point; it does **not** use full rational 2-torsion.

2. The point `8P` supplies one rational root of the 2-division cubic. It does **not** make the residual quadratic split. Up to the square normalization used in `KubertBridgeN16.lean`, the condition

   ```text
   eta^2 = discQ16bc b c
   ```

   is the condition that the residual quadratic split, hence that the other two 2-torsion points are rational. It is an extra `ℤ/2` hypothesis.

3. There is no nonconstant rational map from the cyclic modular curve `X₁(16)` to

$$
E₀: w^2=u^3-u^2-u.
$$

In particular, there cannot be the requested cyclic-case birational formulas to `E₀`. The existing theorem `EN16_point_of_Phi16_and_disc` lives on the **double cover** obtained by adjoining the square root of the residual 2-torsion discriminant. It cannot be descended after deleting `eta`.

The correct cyclic bridge is instead the explicit birational equivalence with the genus-2 curve

$$
C₁₆: V^2=U(U^2+1)(U^2+2U-1).
$$

Complete formulas, including a simple inverse chart from the raw Kubert coordinates, are below. They are suitable for `field_simp`/`ring` proofs.

## 1. Tate normal form and the exact order-16 relation

Let

$$
E_{b,c}: y^2+(1-c)xy-by=x^3-bx^2, \qquad P=(0,0).
$$

For a point of order greater than 3, the usual Tate-normal-form change of variables is defined over the ground field. Thus a rational point `P` of exact order 16 can be put in this form over `ℚ`, without choosing any second point of order 2.

The discriminant is

$$
Δ(b,c)=b^3(16b^2-8bc^2-20bc+b+c(c-1)^3).
$$

The first multiples are

$$
P=(0,0), \qquad 2P=(b,bc), \qquad 3P=(c,b-c).
$$

Consequently, for an exact 16-torsion point,

$$
b≠0, \qquad c≠0, \qquad b≠c.
$$

Introduce the standard raw Kubert coordinates

$$
r=b/c, \qquad s=c^2/(b-c).
$$

Equivalently,

$$
c=s(r-1), \qquad b=rs(r-1).
$$

### The raw equation

The correct square-free exact-order component is

$$
F_{16}(r,s)=r^3s^2-4r^3s+2r^3+3r^2s^2+2r^2s-2r^2-rs^5+4rs^4-10rs^3+6rs^2-3rs+r+s^4.
$$

For `Δ(b,c) ≠ 0`, with `b=rs(r-1)` and `c=s(r-1)`, Sutherland's exact-order proposition gives

$$
P=(0,0)\text{ has exact order }16 \quad ⇔ \quad F_{16}(r,s)=0.
$$

This is stronger than merely saying that `16P=0`. In the construction, the factors

$$
s, \qquad r-1, \qquad F_8(r,s)=rs-2r+1
$$

are removed before taking the square-free order-16 component. Thus `F₁₆=0` is not the order-8 component with a different name.

One derivation uses `9P=-7P`, hence `x(9P)=x(7P)`. The relevant formulas are

$$
x(7P)=rs(r-1)(s-1)(rs-2r+1)/(r-s)^2,
$$

$$
x(9P)=s(r-1)(rs-2r+1)(rs^2-3rs+r+s^2)/(r-s^2+s-1)^2.
$$

After clearing denominators and saturating by the degenerate and lower-order factors, the remaining square-free factor is exactly `F₁₆`.

### A direct polynomial in the Tate coefficients `b,c`

For a denominator-free polynomial directly in the coefficients of `E_{b,c}`, define

$$
\widehat Φ_{16}(b,c):=c^3(b-c)^5 F_{16}(b/c,c^2/(b-c)).
$$

It is the following integral polynomial:

$$
\widehat Φ_{16}(b,c)=-bc^{12}+(b-c)(4b+c)c^{10}-10b(b-c)^2c^8+b(b-c)^3(b^2+3bc+6c^2)c^4-b(b-c)^4(4b^2-2bc+3c^2)c^2+b(b-c)^5(2b^2-2bc+c^2).
$$

Therefore, under `c ≠ 0` and `b-c ≠ 0`,

$$
\widehat Φ_{16}(b,c)=0 \quad ⇔ \quad F_{16}(b/c,c^2/(b-c))=0.
$$

There is another useful normalization. Put `d=b/c`, while retaining `c` as the Tate coefficient, and define

$$
Ψ_{16}(d,c)=-dc^5+(d-1)(4d+1)c^4-10d(d-1)^2c^3+d(d-1)^3(d^2+3d+6)c^2-d(d-1)^4(4d^2-2d+3)c+d(d-1)^5(2d^2-2d+1).
$$

Then

$$
Ψ_{16}(d,s(d-1))=(d-1)^5F_{16}(d,s),
$$

and

$$
\widehat Φ_{16}(b,c)=c^8Ψ_{16}(b/c,c).
$$

These are plain polynomial identities. The fastest audit of the repository definition is:

* unfold `Phi16`;
* compare it to either `widehatPhi16` or `Psi16` above;
* prove the normalization with `field_simp` using `c ≠ 0` and `b-c ≠ 0`, followed by `ring`.

If the current `Phi16` still contains the factor corresponding to `F₈`, then it is an unsaturated `16P=0` equation, not the exact-order `X₁(16)` equation.

A direct Lean-style definition of the canonical raw polynomial is:

```lean
def F16rs (r s : ℚ) : ℚ :=
    r^3 * s^2 - 4 * r^3 * s + 2 * r^3
  + 3 * r^2 * s^2 + 2 * r^2 * s - 2 * r^2
  - r * s^5 + 4 * r * s^4 - 10 * r * s^3
  + 6 * r * s^2 - 3 * r * s + r + s^4
```

## 2. What `8P` gives, and what it does not give

For a general Weierstrass equation, set

$$
Y=2y+a_1x+a_3.
$$

For the Tate model,

$$
a_1=1-c, \qquad a_2=-b, \qquad a_3=-b, \qquad a_4=a_6=0,
$$

so

$$
Y^2=f_2(X):=4X^3+B_2X^2+2B_4X+B_6,
$$

where

$$
B_2=(1-c)^2-4b, \qquad B_4=b(c-1), \qquad B_6=b^2.
$$

The roots of `f₂` are the x-coordinates of the three nonzero geometric 2-torsion points.

For a cyclic point `P` of order 16, the point `8P` is rational. Thus

$$
α:=x(8P)∈ℚ
$$

is one rational root of `f₂`. In the raw coordinates one may take

$$
α=r(r-1)(r-s)(r-s^2+s-1)/(rs-2r+1)^2.
$$

The denominator is nonzero on the exact order-16 locus because `rs-2r+1=0` is the order-8 component.

Factor

$$
f_2(X)=(X-α)Q_{16}(X),
$$

where `Q₁₆` is quadratic over `ℚ`. The cyclic datum proves only that this factorization has coefficients in `ℚ`. It does not prove that `Q₁₆` has a rational root.

For a separable cubic,

$$
Disc(f_2)=Q_{16}(α)^2 Disc(Q_{16}).
$$

Therefore, in square classes,

$$
[Disc(f_2)]=[Disc(Q_{16})].
$$

Moreover,

$$
Disc(Q_{16})\text{ is a square in }ℚ
$$

if and only if the residual quadratic splits over `ℚ`, if and only if the other two nonzero 2-torsion points are rational. In that case there is an independent point `T ∈ E[2](ℚ)` with `T ≠ 8P`, and

$$
⟨P,T⟩≅ℤ/16ℤ×ℤ/2ℤ.
$$

Thus, up to the square factor cleared in `discQ16bc`, the hypothesis

```text
eta^2 = discQ16bc b c
```

is exactly the **extra full-2-torsion condition**. It is not a consequence of the existence of `8P`.

A particularly relevant counterexample is the proposed target curve itself:

$$
w^2=u(u^2-u-1).
$$

It has the rational 2-torsion root `u=0`, but the residual quadratic `u²-u-1` has discriminant 5, which is not a square in `ℚ`. One rational root of a cubic does not make its discriminant a square.

### What replaces the square condition in the cyclic case?

There is no additional square condition on the cyclic moduli problem. The cyclic moduli curve is already a genus-2 double cover of a projective line. Its intrinsic square coordinate is

$$
V^2=U(U^2+1)(U^2+2U-1),
$$

as derived below. This square is obtained by normalizing the order-16 equation itself. It is unrelated to splitting the residual 2-division quadratic.

## 3. Why the requested map to `w²=u³-u²-u` cannot exist

The smooth projective completion of the cyclic order-16 locus is `X₁(16)`, of genus 2. The target `E₀` is an elliptic curve, of genus 1. Hence the two curves are not birational.

More strongly, there is no nonconstant rational map

$$
X_1(16) ⇢ E₀
$$

over `ℚ`.

Since `E₀` is proper, a rational map from the smooth projective curve `X₁(16)` extends to a morphism. Choose a rational cusp as base point and translate `E₀` so that the cusp maps to the identity. A nonconstant morphism would induce a nonzero homomorphism

$$
J_1(16) → E₀.
$$

Its image would be all of `E₀`, so `E₀` would be an isogeny quotient of `J₁(16)`.

The modular curve `X₁(16)`, and therefore `J₁(16)`, has good reduction at every odd prime. In particular it has good reduction at 5, and every abelian quotient of it has good reduction at 5.

For

$$
E₀: w^2=u^3-u^2-u,
$$

the standard invariants are

$$
c_4=64, \qquad Δ=80, \qquad j=c_4^3/Δ=16384/5.
$$

The j-invariant is not 5-adically integral, so `E₀` has bad reduction at 5. This contradicts its being an isogeny quotient of `J₁(16)`.

Consequently, no collection of rational functions in the pure cyclic Tate data can produce a nonconstant point of `E₀` and be verified by a global `ring` identity. Such formulas would define precisely the impossible rational map above.

This also explains the existing theorem. The locus

$$
F_{16}(r,s)=0, \qquad η^2=discQ16bc(r,s)
$$

is a different curve: a degree-2 cover of `X₁(16)` carrying the choice of the extra rational 2-torsion. That cover may map to `E₀`. The map need not, and in this case cannot, descend through the deck involution `η ↦ -η`.

## 4. The correct explicit cyclic bridge: Tate data to the genus-2 `X₁(16)`

### 4.1 Sutherland's optimized plane model

An optimized affine model of `Y₁(16)` is

$$
C_{opt}: y^2+(x^3+x^2-x+1)y+x^2=0.
$$

A point `(x,y)` on this curve maps to the raw Kubert coordinates by

$$
r=(x^2-xy+y^2+y)/(x^2+x-y-1), \qquad s=(x-y)/(x+1).
$$

Then

$$
c=s(r-1), \qquad b=rs(r-1).
$$

These are the standard `X₁(16)` to Tate-normal-form formulas.

### 4.2 A simple inverse chart from `(r,s)`

Put

$$
q=1-s, \qquad a=r-1+s-s^2, \qquad e=2r-rs-1.
$$

On the chart `a ≠ 0`, define

$$
x=(1-s)(r-s)/(r-1+s-s^2), \qquad y=(1-s)x-s.
$$

The following are direct rational identities:

$$
x+1=e/a,
$$

$$
x-y=s(x+1),
$$

$$
x^2+x-y-1=(1-s)^3e/a^2,
$$

$$
x^2-xy+y^2+y=r(x^2+x-y-1),
$$

and, most importantly,

$$
a^4(y^2+(x^3+x^2-x+1)y+x^2)=(1-s)^2eF_{16}(r,s).
$$

Thus `F₁₆(r,s)=0` gives a point on `Copt`, and the two forward fractions recover the original `r,s`, whenever their denominators are nonzero.

This is especially convenient in Lean: prove the last numerator identity once by `field_simp` and `ring`, then rewrite by `hF : F16rs r s = 0`.

```lean
def a16 (r s : ℚ) : ℚ := r - 1 + s - s^2
def e16 (r s : ℚ) : ℚ := 2*r - r*s - 1

def x16 (r s : ℚ) : ℚ :=
  (1 - s) * (r - s) / a16 r s

def y16 (r s : ℚ) : ℚ :=
  (1 - s) * x16 r s - s
```

The exceptional equations `s=1`, `a=0`, and `e=0` are boundary-chart equations. On `F₁₆=0`, their surviving points are cuspidal or degenerate. For example,

$$
F_{16}(r,1)=-(r-1)^3.
$$

For an exact order-16 point they are excluded by nonsingularity and exactness. In Lean, prove these denominator lemmas separately by substituting the vanishing equation into `F16rs` and the Tate discriminant.

### 4.3 Completion of the square

Let

$$
A(x)=x^3+x^2-x+1, \qquad z=2y+A(x).
$$

Then

$$
z^2=A(x)^2-4x^2=(x^2-1)(x^2+1)(x^2+2x-1).
$$

The polynomial verification is

$$
z^2-(x^2-1)(x^2+1)(x^2+2x-1)=4(y^2+A(x)y+x^2).
$$

### 4.4 The standard hyperelliptic model

Set

$$
U=(1-x)/(1+x), \qquad V=2z/(1+x)^3.
$$

Then

$$
V^2=U(U^2+1)(U^2+2U-1).
$$

The inverse formulas are

$$
x=(1-U)/(1+U), \qquad z=4V/(1+U)^3, \qquad y=(z-(x^3+x^2-x+1))/2.
$$

Combining the stages gives an explicit rational-function bridge from the Tate data:

$$
r=b/c, \qquad s=c^2/(b-c),
$$

$$
x=(1-s)(r-s)/(r-1+s-s^2), \qquad y=(1-s)x-s,
$$

$$
z=2y+x^3+x^2-x+1, \qquad U=(1-x)/(1+x), \qquad V=2z/(1+x)^3.
$$

The first coordinate simplifies to

$$
U=(rs-1+2s-2s^2)/(2r-rs-1).
$$

For Lean, keeping `V` in the staged form through `x,y,z` is preferable to expanding its numerator. Every verification is then a small `field_simp` followed by `ring`.

### Suggested algebraic lemma sequence

```lean
-- 1. Raw order-16 point gives the optimized model.
lemma opt_identity (r s : ℚ) (ha : a16 r s ≠ 0) :
    (a16 r s)^4 *
      (y16 r s ^ 2
       + ((x16 r s)^3 + (x16 r s)^2 - x16 r s + 1) * y16 r s
       + (x16 r s)^2)
      = (1 - s)^2 * e16 r s * F16rs r s := by
  field_simp [x16, y16, a16, e16, ha]
  ring

-- 2. Complete the square.
lemma complete_square_identity (x y : ℚ) :
    let A := x^3 + x^2 - x + 1
    (2*y + A)^2 - (x^2 - 1)*(x^2 + 1)*(x^2 + 2*x - 1)
      = 4 * (y^2 + A*y + x^2) := by
  dsimp
  ring

-- 3. Apply the Möbius change of variables.
-- Prove after field_simp [hx1] and ring.
```

The first script may need minor syntactic adjustment for the repository's simplifier normal form; the mathematical identity is the displayed identity above.

## 5. Cusps on the correct cyclic model

For

$$
C_{16}: V^2=U(U^2+1)(U^2+2U-1),
$$

the open modular curve `Y₁(16)` is obtained by deleting the points whose U-coordinate satisfies

$$
U(U-1)(U+1)(U^2+1)(U^2-2U-1)(U^2+2U-1)=0,
$$

and by deleting the point over `U=∞`.

Thus the geometric cusp U-values are

$$
U=∞, \quad 0, \quad ±1, \quad U^2+1=0, \quad U^2-2U-1=0, \quad U^2+2U-1=0.
$$

The rational cusps are the six rational points

$$
∞, \qquad (0,0), \qquad (1,2), (1,-2), \qquad (-1,2), (-1,-2).
$$

In the optimized `(x,y)` chart, the finite rational cusp representatives include

$$
(1,-1), \qquad (0,0), \qquad (0,-1), \qquad (-1,-1),
$$

with the remaining two represented at infinity in that plane model.

There are no corresponding “target u-values” on `w²=u³-u²-u` for the pure cyclic problem, because there is no cyclic-case map to that elliptic curve. The U-values above are the cusp values for the correct genus-2 obstruction.

Determining that `C₁₆(ℚ)` consists only of these cusps is the genuine downstream theorem needed to exclude rational cyclic 16-torsion. A proof about rational points on `E₀` cannot replace it without the extra discriminant-square hypothesis.

## 6. Recommended change to the Lean architecture

The clean separation is:

1. **Cyclic layer.** From a rational exact order-16 point, construct Tate `b,c`, prove the exact raw equation `F₁₆(r,s)=0`, and construct a noncuspidal rational point on

   $$
   V^2=U(U^2+1)(U^2+2U-1).
   $$

2. **Extra-2-torsion layer.** Retain

   ```text
   EN16_point_of_Phi16_and_disc
   ```

   only for the stronger hypotheses `Phi16 = 0` and `eta^2 = discQ16bc`. Its input moduli problem is the full-2-torsion cover, not `X₁(16)`.

3. Do not add a lemma claiming that `x(8 • P)` makes `discQ16bc` a square. The correct lemma says only that the 2-division cubic has one rational root and therefore factors as a rational linear factor times a rational quadratic.

A suitable new theorem should look conceptually like

```lean
theorem X1N16_point_of_Phi16
    (b c : ℚ)
    (hdisc : tateDisc b c ≠ 0)
    (h16 : Phi16 b c = 0)
    (hexact : -- exclusions if Phi16 was not already saturated) :
    ∃ U V : ℚ,
      V^2 = U * (U^2 + 1) * (U^2 + 2*U - 1) ∧
      U * (U - 1) * (U + 1)
        * (U^2 + 1) * (U^2 - 2*U - 1) * (U^2 + 2*U - 1) ≠ 0 := by
  ...
```

The formulas in Section 4 supply the body of this theorem. The remaining work is denominator/cusp bookkeeping, not another elliptic-curve birational transformation.
