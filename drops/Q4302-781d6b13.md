# Rational \(35\)-torsion: the useful reduction is \(X_1(35)	o X_0(35)	o 35\mathrm a1\)

## Executive conclusion

1. **The genus is exactly**
   
   \[
   g\bigl(X_1(35)\bigr)=25.
   \]

2. Putting the \(7\)-torsion point at the Tate origin gives the standard Kubert family

   \[
   E_t:\quad y^2+(1+t-t^2)xy-t^2(t-1)y=x^3-t^2(t-1)x^2,
   \qquad P_7=(0,0).
   \]

   The \(x\)-coordinate \(X\) of a geometric \(5\)-torsion point satisfies an explicit degree-\(12\) division-polynomial equation

   \[
   G_{35}(t,X)=\psi_5(E_t;X)=0.
   \]

   There is, however, an important rationality caveat: **\(G_{35}(t,X)=0\) with \(t,X\in\mathbf Q\) only gives a rational \(x\)-coordinate**. To assert that the original \(E_t\), rather than a quadratic twist, has a rational \(5\)-torsion point, one must also impose the Weierstrass square equation

   \[
   V^2=4X^3+b_2(t)X^2+2b_4(t)X+b_6(t).
   \]

   The normalization of this two-equation system, after deleting the discriminant/cusp locus, is the correct exact-\(35\) cover.

3. There **is** a clean lower-genus route. The forgetful map

   \[
   X_1(35)\longrightarrow X_0(35)
   \]

   has degree \(\varphi(35)/2=12\). The genus-\(3\) curve \(X_0(35)\) has an explicit Atkin--Lehner quotient of genus \(1\), birational to

   \[
   E_{35\mathrm a1}:\qquad Y^2+Y=X^3+X^2+9X+1.
   \]

   This elliptic curve has

   \[
   E_{35\mathrm a1}(\mathbf Q)\cong \mathbf Z/3\mathbf Z,
   \]

   and its three rational points correspond on the quotient quartic to two cusp images and one point whose fiber on \(X_0(35)\) is defined over \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt5)\), not over \(\mathbf Q\). Consequently \(X_0(35)(\mathbf Q)\) consists of its four cusps, and hence \(X_1(35)\) has no noncuspidal rational point.

4. **Difficulty assessment:** the direct \((t,X,V)\) equation is much worse than the equations used for \(15,18,21\). But the quotient proof is **closer to the \(15/21\) genus-\(1\) strategy than to the \(18\) genus-\(2\)-Jacobian strategy**. It is not a one-line Fermat factorization: the arithmetic core is a rank-\(0\) proof for the small elliptic curve \(35\mathrm a1\), naturally done by a \(3\)-isogeny descent. No genus-\(2\) or genus-\(25\) Jacobian computation is intrinsically required.

## 1. Genus bookkeeping for \(X_1(35)\)

For \(N>2\), use the index in \(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbf Z)\):

\[
\mu_1(N)
 =\frac12N^2\prod_{p\mid N}\left(1-\frac1{p^2}\right).
\]

For \(N=35=5\cdot7\),

\[
\mu_1(35)
 =\frac12\cdot35^2\cdot\frac{24}{25}\cdot\frac{48}{49}
 =576.
\]

Since \(\Gamma_1(N)\) is torsion-free for \(N\ge5\), there are no elliptic points. The cusp count is

\[
\begin{aligned}
c_1(35)
 &=\frac12\sum_{d\mid35}\varphi(d)\varphi(35/d)\\
 &=\frac12(24+24+24+24)=48.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
g(X_1(35))
 &=1+\frac{\mu_1(35)}{12}-\frac{c_1(35)}2\\
 &=1+48-24\\
 &=25.
\end{aligned}
\]

So the guess “around \(25\)” is exactly right. Derickx--Etropolski--van Hoeij--Morrow--Zureick-Brown also list \(X_1(35)\) with genus \(25\), and their treatment proceeds through \(X_0(35)\).

## 2. The explicit Tate/division-polynomial construction

### 2.1 Order \(7\) at the Tate origin

Start with Tate normal form

\[
E(b,c):\quad y^2+(1-c)xy-by=x^3-bx^2,
\qquad P=(0,0).
\]

Kubert's order-\(7\) condition is parametrized by

\[
b=t^3-t^2=t^2(t-1),
\qquad
c=t^2-t=t(t-1).
\]

Thus

\[
E_t:\quad
 y^2+(1+t-t^2)xy-t^2(t-1)y
 =x^3-t^2(t-1)x^2,
\]

with \(P_7=(0,0)\) of exact order \(7\), away from the zeros of the discriminant.

The generalized Weierstrass coefficients are

```text
a1 = 1 + t - t^2
a2 = -t^2(t-1)
a3 = -t^2(t-1)
a4 = 0
a6 = 0
```

and hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
b_2(t)&=a_1^2+4a_2
      =t^4-6t^3+3t^2+2t+1,\\
b_4(t)&=a_1a_3
      =t^2(t-1)(t^2-t-1),\\
b_6(t)&=a_3^2
      =t^4(t-1)^2,\\
b_8(t)&=a_2a_3^2
      =-t^6(t-1)^3.
\end{aligned}
\]

### 2.2 A compact explicit formula for \(G_{35}(t,X)\)

Let

\[
D_2(t,X)=4X^3+b_2X^2+2b_4X+b_6.
\]

This is \(\psi_2^2\) after eliminating \(y\). Also put

\[
D_3(t,X)
 =3X^4+b_2X^3+3b_4X^2+3b_6X+b_8
 =\psi_3(E_t;X),
\]

and

\[
\begin{aligned}
H_4(t,X)={}&2X^6+b_2X^5+5b_4X^4+10b_6X^3+10b_8X^2\\
&+(b_2b_8-b_4b_6)X+(b_4b_8-b_6^2),
\end{aligned}
\]

so that \(\psi_4=\psi_2H_4\). The odd division-polynomial recurrence gives

\[
\psi_5=\psi_4\psi_2^3-\psi_3^3.
\]

Consequently the desired bivariate polynomial can be written without an enormous full expansion as

\[
\boxed{
G_{35}(t,X)
  =D_2(t,X)^2H_4(t,X)-D_3(t,X)^3.
}
\]

It lies in \(\mathbf Z[t,X]\) and has degree \(12\) in \(X\). This is the structurally useful form for Lean: all pieces are small, and the identity with \(\psi_5\) follows from the standard division-polynomial recurrence rather than a giant `ring` expansion.

In pseudocode:

```text
b2 := t^4 - 6*t^3 + 3*t^2 + 2*t + 1;
b4 := t^2*(t-1)*(t^2-t-1);
b6 := t^4*(t-1)^2;
b8 := -t^6*(t-1)^3;

D2 := 4*X^3 + b2*X^2 + 2*b4*X + b6;
D3 := 3*X^4 + b2*X^3 + 3*b4*X^2 + 3*b6*X + b8;
H4 := 2*X^6 + b2*X^5 + 5*b4*X^4 + 10*b6*X^3
      + 10*b8*X^2 + (b2*b8-b4*b6)*X + (b4*b8-b6^2);
G35 := D2^2*H4 - D3^3;
```

### 2.3 Why \(G_{35}(t,X)=0\) alone is not yet exact \(35\)-torsion

For a point \(Q=(X,Y)\) on a generalized Weierstrass equation, set

\[
V=2Y+a_1X+a_3.
\]

Then the curve equation is equivalent to

\[
V^2=D_2(t,X).
\]

Thus an actual rational \(5\)-torsion point on the fixed curve \(E_t/\mathbf Q\) gives

\[
\boxed{
G_{35}(t,X)=0,
\qquad
V^2=D_2(t,X),
\qquad
\Delta(E_t)\ne0.
}
\]

Conversely, a rational solution of this system gives

\[
Y=\frac{V-a_1X-a_3}{2}\in\mathbf Q,
\]

and \(Q=(X,Y)\) is a nonzero point killed by \(5\), hence has exact order \(5\). Together with \(P_7\), it generates a cyclic subgroup of order \(35\).

The distinction matters. A rational root \(X\) of \(\psi_5\) can have

\[
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf Q}/\mathbf Q)\cdot Q
 \subseteq\{Q,-Q\}
\]

without either \(Q\) or \(-Q\) being rational. Equivalently, it can record rational \(5\)-level structure only after a quadratic twist. Since the Tate normalization has fixed a rational \(7\)-torsion point on the **original** curve, one may not silently twist the \(5\)-part independently. Therefore:

> The plane curve \(G_{35}(t,X)=0\) is an excellent necessary-condition quotient, but the exact same-curve \(5+7\) moduli problem includes the square equation \(V^2=D_2(t,X)\).

After normalization and removal of bad fibers, the complete system is the relevant \(X_1(35)\) cover. Attacking its rational points directly is possible in principle but is the wrong route: the degree-\(12\) equation is highly singular as a plane model and conceals the much smaller modular quotients.

## 3. The decisive quotient route

### 3.1 First forget the generator: \(X_1(35)\to X_0(35)\)

A rational point \(P_{35}\in E(\mathbf Q)\) makes the cyclic subgroup

\[
C=\langle P_{35}\rangle
\]

Galois-stable. Therefore it determines a rational noncuspidal point on \(X_0(35)\). The forgetful map has degree

\[
[\Gamma_0(35):\{\pm1\}\Gamma_1(35)]
 =\frac{\varphi(35)}2=12.
\]

Hence it is enough to prove

\[
X_0(35)(\mathbf Q)=\{\text{cusps}\}.
\]

This already replaces a genus-\(25\) problem by a genus-\(3\) problem.

### 3.2 A genus-\(3\) hyperelliptic model

A standard model is

\[
H:\quad w^2=f(z),
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
f(z)={}&z^8-4z^7-6z^6-4z^5-9z^4\\
      &\quad+4z^3-6z^2+4z+1.
\end{aligned}
}
\]

The four rational cusps are

\[
(0,1),\quad(0,-1),\quad\infty_+,\quad\infty_-.
\]

The existing genus-\(3\) proof computes

\[
J_0(35)(\mathbf Q)\cong \mathbf Z/24\mathbf Z\oplus\mathbf Z/2\mathbf Z
\]

and rank \(0\). In the public verification code, reduction modulo \(3\) gives a group of order \(48\), the cuspidal subgroup already has order \(48\), and a \(2\)-Selmer computation gives the rank upper bound \(0\). Riemann--Roch enumeration of the resulting finite divisor classes then shows that the four displayed cusps are all of \(H(\mathbf Q)\).

That route is rigorous, but there is an even smaller quotient which answers the “is it elementary like \(15/21\)?” question positively.

### 3.3 The hidden genus-\(1\) quotient

The polynomial satisfies

\[
f(z)=z^8f(-1/z).
\]

Therefore \(H\) has the involution

\[
\iota:(z,w)\longmapsto\left(-\frac1z,\frac{w}{z^4}\right).
\]

On this model, \(\iota\) is one of the two proper Atkin--Lehner involutions \(w_5,w_7\); which label is assigned is irrelevant here. The hyperelliptic involution is \(w_{35}\).

Take invariant functions

\[
u=z-\frac1z,
\qquad
v=\frac{w}{z^2}.
\]

Using

\[
\begin{aligned}
z^2+z^{-2}&=u^2+2,\\
z^3-z^{-3}&=u^3+3u,\\
z^4+z^{-4}&=u^4+4u^2+2,
\end{aligned}
\]

one obtains the quotient curve

\[
\boxed{
C:\quad
v^2=u^4-4u^3-2u^2-16u-19.
}
\]

The quartic factors at one rational root:

\[
u^4-4u^3-2u^2-16u-19
 =(u+1)(u^3-5u^2+3u-19),
\]

so \(C\) is a genus-\(1\) curve with rational point \((-1,0)\).

#### Birational reduction to conductor \(35\)

The following elementary substitutions exhibit its Jacobian explicitly. Put

\[
s=\frac1{u+1},
\qquad
W=\frac{v}{(u+1)^2}.
\]

Then

\[
W^2=1-8s+16s^2-28s^3.
\]

Set

\[
Z=W-1+4s,
\qquad
X=\frac{s}{Z},
\qquad
Y=\frac1Z.
\]

The equation becomes

\[
2Y^2+(1-8X)Y+28X^3=0.
\]

With

\[
x_0=-14X,
\qquad
y_0=14Y,
\]

this is

\[
y_0^2+4x_0y_0+7y_0=x_0^3.
\]

Finally, the change

\[
x_0=x-1,
\qquad
y_0=y-2x-1
\]

gives

\[
\boxed{
E:\quad y^2+y=x^3+x^2+9x+1.
}
\]

This is Cremona/LMFDB isogeny class \(35\mathrm a\), curve \(35\mathrm a1\). Its Mordell--Weil group is

\[
E(\mathbf Q)=\{O,(1,3),(1,-4)\}
 \cong\mathbf Z/3\mathbf Z.
\]

A standard rational \(3\)-isogeny descent on the equivalent model

\[
y_0^2+4x_0y_0+7y_0=x_0^3
\]

proves rank \(0\); \((0,0)\) and \((0,-7)\) are the two nonzero rational \(3\)-torsion points. This is the only genuine arithmetic input needed in the elliptic-quotient proof.

#### Enumerating the fibers

Because \(C\) is birational to \(E\) and \(E(\mathbf Q)\) has three points, the complete set is

\[
C(\mathbf Q)=\{\infty_+,\infty_-,(-1,0)\}.
\]

The two points at infinity have as their inverse images on \(H\) precisely the four rational cusps

\[
(0,\pm1),\quad\infty_\pm.
\]

For the remaining quotient point \((-1,0)\), a lift must satisfy

\[
z-\frac1z=-1,
\]

or equivalently

\[
z^2+z-1=0.
\]

Thus

\[
z=\frac{-1\pm\sqrt5}{2},
\qquad w=0.
\]

These are two quadratic points, not rational points. They are the familiar CM points of discriminant \(-35\) on this model.

It follows immediately that

\[
H(\mathbf Q)=\{(0,\pm1),\infty_+,\infty_-\},
\]

so every rational point of \(X_0(35)\) is cuspidal.

### 3.4 The complete contradiction

The proof can now be compressed to the following chain:

```text
P35 in E(Q)
  => C = <P35> is a Q-rational cyclic subgroup of order 35
  => a noncuspidal Q-point of X0(35)
  => a Q-point of C = X0(35)/<iota>
  => one of infinity+, infinity-, (-1,0)
  => either the X0(35)-point is a cusp,
     or its z-coordinate lies in Q(sqrt(5)) \ Q
  => contradiction.
```

This proves the desired exclusion without analyzing \(X_1(35)\) or its Jacobian.

## 4. What the Jacobian factorization is saying

Since \(g(X_0(35))=3\), its Jacobian has dimension \(3\). Up to \(\mathbf Q\)-isogeny it decomposes as

\[
J_0(35)\sim_{\mathbf Q} E_{35\mathrm a}\times A_2,
\]

where

- \(E_{35\mathrm a}\) is the one-dimensional factor above; and
- \(A_2\) is a modular abelian surface coming from the quadratic newform orbit at level \(35\).

Both factors have rank \(0\). The Atkin--Lehner quotient \(H\to C\simeq E_{35\mathrm a}\) isolates the elliptic factor. The full genus-\(3\) \(2\)-Selmer computation controls \(E_{35\mathrm a}\times A_2\), but for rational **points on the curve**, the elliptic quotient plus the explicit fiber check already suffices. In particular, one does not need to determine all of \(A_2(\mathbf Q)\) merely to exclude rational \(35\)-torsion.

This explains why \(X_0(35)\) helps much more than its genus \(3\) initially suggests.

## 5. Difficulty relative to \(15,21,18\)

| order | natural direct model | useful smaller quotient | realistic arithmetic core |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| \(15=3\cdot5\) | small division-polynomial curve | genus \(1\) | rank-\(0\) elliptic curve |
| \(21=3\cdot7\) | small Tate/division curve | genus \(1\) quotient | rank-\(0\) elliptic curve |
| \(18=2\cdot3^2\) | genus \(2\) model | no comparably decisive elliptic quotient in the current route | genus-\(2\) Jacobian/descent or a special Diophantine shortcut |
| \(35=5\cdot7\) | exact cover of a degree-\(12\) \(\psi_5\) plane curve; \(g(X_1)=25\) | \(X_0(35)\) of genus \(3\), then \(35\mathrm a1\) of genus \(1\) | rank-\(0\) \(3\)-isogeny descent plus a quadratic fiber check |

So there are two different answers depending on which model one insists on using:

- **Direct \(G_{35}(t,X)\) approach:** hard and unattractive; substantially worse than \(18\).
- **Modular quotient approach:** moderate and essentially elliptic; closer to \(15/21\), and arguably cleaner than \(18\).

It is not a Fermat/Catalan-style terminal equation. The terminal obstruction is instead

\[
E_{35\mathrm a1}(\mathbf Q)=\mathbf Z/3\mathbf Z
\]

followed by the elementary discriminant-\(5\) lift calculation.

## 6. Recommended formalization seam

For a Lean-oriented proof, I would not normalize the full \(G_{35}\)-surface and then prove its rational points. Bank the following four interfaces instead.

### Seam A: torsion to a \(35\)-isogeny

```text
rational point of exact order 35
  -> rational cyclic subgroup of order 35
  -> noncuspidal rational X0(35)-point.
```

This is elementary group/Galois bookkeeping.

### Seam B: explicit \(X_0(35)\) model and involution

Bank the model

```text
w^2 = z^8 - 4*z^7 - 6*z^6 - 4*z^5 - 9*z^4
      + 4*z^3 - 6*z^2 + 4*z + 1
```

and verify by polynomial identities that

```text
(z,w) |-> (-1/z, w/z^4)
u = z - 1/z
v = w/z^2
```

lands on

```text
v^2 = u^4 - 4*u^3 - 2*u^2 - 16*u - 19.
```

The algebra here is entirely `field_simp`/`ring` territory.

### Seam C: the elliptic rank-\(0\) lemma

Prove or import a certified descent theorem giving

```text
E(Q) = {O, (1,3), (1,-4)}
for E : y^2 + y = x^3 + x^2 + 9*x + 1.
```

A \(3\)-isogeny descent is the natural elementary proof. This is a much smaller formalization target than a genus-\(2\) or genus-\(3\) Jacobian descent.

### Seam D: inverse-fiber calculation

The only noncusp quotient point gives

```text
u = -1
z^2 + z - 1 = 0
```

whose discriminant is \(5\), so there is no rational lift. The other two quotient points lift only to the four cusps.

Once these are banked, the order-\(35\) contradiction is a short theorem. The direct Tate polynomial \(G_{35}\) remains useful as a cross-check and as an explicit bridge if the project insists on staying entirely in Weierstrass coordinates, but it should not be the rational-point engine.

## Final verdict

**\(35\) is not intrinsically a genus-\(25\) problem.** Its direct Tate/division-polynomial presentation is large, but the modular quotient chain

\[
X_1(35)\longrightarrow X_0(35)
 \longrightarrow X_0(35)/\langle w_5\text{ or }w_7\rangle
 \simeq 35\mathrm a1
\]

reduces the rational-torsion exclusion to a rank-\(0\) elliptic curve and the elementary equation \(z^2+z-1=0\). For the Mazur formalization seam, this should be classified as **elliptic-quotient manageable, closer to \(15/21\) than to \(18\)**.
