# Remaining axioms after the N18 port: mathematical status and formalization roadmap

## Executive verdict

Assuming the port removes `no_order_18` from

```lean
#print axioms mazur_cyclic_order_bound_assembled
```

the remaining seven assumptions are all **true established mathematical facts**. None is an open problem. The important distinction is not truth versus uncertainty, but the kind of formal debt each axiom represents:

1. **Small explicit algebra blocked mainly by a missing reusable API**
   - `exists_rational_two_isogeny_quotient`.
2. **Fixed low-genus arithmetic, substantial but realistically formalizable with explicit certificates**
   - `no_order_19_prime`;
   - `no_order_17_prime`;
   - `no_raw_order49_tate_obstruction`;
   - `no_order_13_prime`.
3. **Large modular-curve arithmetic whose known proof uses infrastructure far beyond current Mathlib/project support**
   - `no_explicit_order25_obstruction`;
   - `no_prime_order_ge_23`.

The categories in the question are not disjoint. For example, `no_order_13_prime` is a theorem proved in the literature, requires a serious new formal proof, and also needs Jacobian/descent infrastructure that is not supplied by Mathlib. The useful audit therefore has two axes:

- **mathematical status:** known theorem versus project-specific corollary;
- **formal bottleneck:** finite ring certificates, fixed descent, or missing modular/Jacobian infrastructure.

A sensible elimination order is

```text
exists_rational_two_isogeny_quotient
  -> no_order_19_prime
  -> no_order_17_prime
  -> no_raw_order49_tate_obstruction
  -> no_order_13_prime
  -> no_explicit_order25_obstruction
  -> no_prime_order_ge_23.
```

The middle three can share an elliptic rank-zero/reduction package. The N18 port makes the N13 route much more plausible because it supplies reusable genus-two Picard, weak-descent, filtration, and separatedness components. The final two are qualitatively larger projects.

# 1. `no_order_13_prime`

## Mathematical status

This is a classical consequence of Mazur's torsion theorem, and it also has a completely fixed modular-curve formulation independent of the general theorem:

```text
X_1(13)^opt : v^2 + (u^3 + u^2 + 1)v = u^2 + u
```

has only the six rational cusp points: four affine points above `u=0,-1` and the two points at infinity. The Jacobian is a genus-two Jacobian with

```text
rank J_1(13)(Q) = 0,
J_1(13)(Q) ~= Z/19Z.
```

So this is not new mathematics. The exact theorem needed by the project is a fixed rational-point computation.

## Is there a genuinely elementary shortcut?

There is no comparably tiny `X_0(13)` shortcut. `X_0(13)` has genus zero and infinitely many rational points representing rational 13-isogenies. The missing condition is that the kernel line possess a **rational generator**, not merely be Galois stable. Passing to `X_0(13)` therefore loses exactly the information one needs.

One can package historical proofs in several ways, but each eventually proves the same genus-two arithmetic statement. A direct congruence sieve does not work because the curve is locally soluble, including at 2.

## Recommended formal route

Use the route already identified for the optimized sextic model:

1. Convert to the monic even sextic
   ```text
   Y^2 = u^6 + 2u^5 + u^4 + 2u^3 + 6u^2 + 4u + 1.
   ```
2. Construct the genus-two Jacobian/Picard group using the N18 machinery.
3. Perform the **true** even-degree 2-descent, not only the fake `x-T` descent.
4. Prove the weak endpoint
   ```lean
   theorem j13_two_surjective (D : J13Q) : ∃ E, D = 2 • E
   ```
   rather than first proving a rank theorem.
5. At 2, prove that the reduced Jacobian has no nonzero 2-torsion and that the formal kernel has no nonzero infinitely 2-divisible element.
6. Deduce that reduction
   ```text
   J_1(13)(Q) -> J_1(13)(F_2)
   ```
   is injective.
7. Enumerate the six points of `X_1(13)(F_2)`, all reductions of rational cusps, and finish by Abel-Jacobi injectivity.

This avoids proving the stronger isomorphism `J_1(13)(Q) ~= Z/19Z` unless it is independently useful.

## Infrastructure assessment

The finite arithmetic is small: the relevant `S`-squareclass space has only 64 raw classes. The hard theorem is the comparison between explicit even-sextic descent data and the genuine Kummer map

```text
J(Q)/2J(Q) -> H^1(Q,J[2]).
```

Current Mathlib does not provide a ready true genus-two descent package, a computational genus-two Jacobian, or the complete point-reduction/formal-kernel API. The N18 port should remove a large fraction of this debt, but the curve-specific true-descent exactness remains substantial.

**Assessment:** known theorem; serious fixed formalization; substantial missing Jacobian/descent infrastructure. Roughly a few thousand lines unless the N18 abstractions are already sufficiently generic.

Primary descent references include Bruin--Stoll, *Two-cover descent on hyperelliptic curves* (`arXiv:0803.2052`) and Bruin--Poonen--Stoll, *Generalized explicit descent and its application to curves of genus 3* (`arXiv:1205.4456`).

# 2. `no_order_17_prime`

## Mathematical status

This is classical and fixed-level. The economical target is not the genus-five curve `X_1(17)`, but

```text
X_0(17) = 17a1:
  y^2 + xy + y = x^3 - x^2 - x - 14.
```

Its rational group is cyclic of order 4. In the normalization used by the current bridge, the four rational points are

```text
O, (7,13), (11/4,-15/8), (7,-21).
```

Two are cusps and two are noncuspidal 17-isogeny points. Therefore the statement

```text
rank X_0(17)(Q) = 0
```

is not enough: the two noncuspidal fibers must also be excluded.

## Recommended formal route

1. Use the explicit diamond-trace Tate bridge already computed:
   ```text
   F17(b,c)=0 -> (mapX17(b,c),mapY17(b,c)) in X_0(17)(Q).
   ```
2. Use the polynomial cusp certificate to show the image of a smooth exact-order Tate point is not either cusp.
3. Prove the exact rational-point list on `17a1` by a fixed 2-isogeny descent plus formal-kernel separatedness/reduction.
4. For each of the two noncuspidal points, exclude a rational lift to `X_1(17)`.

There are two practical fiber certificates:

- a degree-8 cyclic-kernel polynomial with no rational root;
- a local argument using the two source `j`-invariants and reduction at 3.

The kernel-polynomial route is more directly finite. One generic twist/descent lemma is necessary: equality of `X_0` moduli points determines the represented pair only up to twist. Because the relevant geometric automorphism group is `{+1,-1}`, a rational `X_1` lift forces the `x`-coordinate of a generator on the chosen representative kernel to be rational, hence gives a rational root of the stored kernel polynomial.

## Infrastructure assessment

The curve-specific calculations are finite. The missing reusable pieces are:

- a fixed elliptic 2-isogeny/weak-descent package;
- point reduction and formal-kernel separatedness;
- either a lightweight moduli/fiber theorem or explicit Tate-coordinate bridge;
- total isogeny/kernel data if the kernel polynomial is generated through Vélu.

This is substantially smaller than a formal theory of `X_1(17)` or its Jacobian.

**Assessment:** known theorem; medium fixed formalization; depends on missing but reusable elliptic reduction/isogeny infrastructure.

# 3. `no_order_19_prime`

## Mathematical status

This is parallel to N17 but slightly smaller. Use

```text
X_0(19) = 19a2:
  y^2 + y = x^3 + x^2 - 9x - 15.
```

Its rational group has three points. In the standard normalization there are two cusps and one noncuspidal CM 19-isogeny point. Again, rank zero does not by itself prove the torsion exclusion; the noncuspidal generator fiber must be shown to have no rational point.

## Recommended formal route

1. Prove the fixed weak 2-descent on `19a2`.
2. Use the 2-adic formal kernel to show every rational point is killed by 3.
3. Enumerate the three rational points either by reduction modulo 2 or by the explicit factored 3-division quartic.
4. Evaluate the explicit `X_1(19)->X_0(19)` bridge/cusp orientation.
5. Exclude the unique noncuspidal fiber using its degree-9 kernel polynomial, or an equivalent CM/local certificate.

The last finite no-root proof is genuinely elementary once the representative is fixed. The same generic twist/fiber lemma used for N17 should be reused.

## Infrastructure assessment

The same elliptic point-reduction and two-isogeny infrastructure is needed as for N17. The curve-specific arithmetic is smaller: one noncuspidal fiber rather than two.

**Assessment:** known theorem; medium fixed formalization; likely the easiest prime case after the two-isogeny quotient infrastructure is available.

# 4. `no_prime_order_ge_23`

## Mathematical status

This is the uniform prime tail of Mazur's theorem. It is a true theorem with several classical formulations:

- Mazur's Eisenstein-quotient/formal-immersion argument;
- Oesterlé/Parent-style explicit prime-torsion bounds over number fields;
- a detour through Mazur's prime-isogeny classification followed by fiber exclusions.

For degree one, strong uniform torsion bounds leave no prime above 7. That does not make the formal proof elementary: the proof contains essentially the same modular-curve and formal-immersion machinery.

## Recommended formal route

The honest scalable route is uniform:

```text
rational point of exact prime order p
  -> rational noncuspidal point of X_1(p)
  -> image in X_0(p)
  -> rank-zero Eisenstein quotient of J_0(p)
  -> reduction + torsion injection
  -> formal immersion at the cusp
  -> contradiction for p >= 23.
```

Do not build `J_1(p)` prime by prime. Its dimension grows quadratically. Also, the rational-isogeny detour does not remove the deep theorem: proving the classification of rational prime isogenies is itself a major modular-curve result, and it still leaves exceptional primes `37,43,67,163` whose generator fibers must be treated.

## Infrastructure assessment

This is the largest remaining foundational block. A faithful Lean proof needs substantial parts of:

- modular curves `X_0(p)`/`X_1(p)` and their integral models;
- Jacobians and Abel-Jacobi maps;
- Hecke correspondences and the Eisenstein ideal/quotient;
- cuspidal divisor arithmetic and Manin--Drinfeld input;
- reduction of modular points and torsion injectivity;
- cotangent maps and formal immersion at a cusp;
- enough `q`-expansion/modular-symbol theory to prove the relevant quotient nontrivial and rank zero.

Current Mathlib does not package this theorem. This is not merely a missing tactic lemma; it is a project-scale library development.

**Assessment:** known deep theorem; very substantial new formal mathematics; major missing modular/Hecke/Néron infrastructure. This is likely the final axiom to remove.

Mazur's primary source is *Modular curves and the Eisenstein ideal*, Publ. Math. IHÉS 47 (1977).

# 5. `no_explicit_order25_obstruction`

## Mathematical status

This project-specific proposition is a concrete open-locus version of the known statement that a rational elliptic curve has no rational point of exact order 25. The natural modular curve is `X_1(25)`, whose smooth projective model has

```text
g(X_1(25)) = 12.
```

It is not genus zero and not another N18-style genus-two problem. The known arithmetic is

```text
rank J_1(25)(Q) = 0,
J_1(25)(Q) ~= Z/227555Z,
227555 = 5 * 71 * 641,
#X_1(25)(F_3) = 10,
```

and the ten `F_3` points are reductions of the ten rational cusps.

## Recommended formal route

The published local-method proof has an excellent final shape:

1. Map an `ExplicitOrder25Obstruction` to a noncuspidal rational point of the smooth normalization of the `F25=0` Tate model.
2. Fix a rational cusp `O` and use
   ```text
   P |-> [P-O] in J_1(25)(Q).
   ```
3. Prove a strong rational-Jacobian endpoint, for example
   ```lean
   theorem J25_annihilated (D : J25Q) : 227555 • D = 0
   ```
   or an explicit equivalence with `ZMod 227555`.
4. Since the annihilator is prime to 3, use good reduction to inject the rational Jacobian into the mod-3 Jacobian.
5. Enumerate the ten mod-3 curve points, all cusp reductions.
6. Use Abel-Jacobi injectivity to conclude that every rational point is a cusp.

The Tate-to-normalization bridge and cusp/open-locus certificates are finite algebra, though potentially large.

## Where the real difficulty lies

The final mod-3 argument is short. The difficult theorem is the rational-Jacobian annihilator. The published rank computation uses modular symbols/winding quotients and deep modular-abelian-variety rank results. In a development that deliberately avoids invoking Mordell--Weil finite generation as a black box, “rank zero” is not by itself the usable endpoint.

Three possible proof projects are:

- formalize the fixed modular-symbol/Kato-style rank certificate;
- develop an explicit descent on the 4- and 8-dimensional modular factors;
- prove a direct fixed formal-immersion theorem at the mod-3 cusps.

All are large. A direct port of N18 genus-two code is not available because the Jacobian has dimension 12. A commonly used diamond quotient has genus 4, not genus 2, and does not automatically control all rational points on the full curve.

**Assessment:** known theorem and published computation; very large fixed formalization; major missing high-genus Jacobian/modular-symbol infrastructure. It is probably the second-hardest axiom after the uniform prime tail.

A useful primary computational reference is Derickx--Etropolski--van Hoeij--Morrow--Zureick-Brown, *Sporadic cubic torsion* (`arXiv:2007.13929`), whose accompanying computations include the level-25 rank and torsion certificates.

# 6. `no_raw_order49_tate_obstruction`

## Mathematical status

This is a project-specific Tate-normal-form consequence of the fixed theorem

```text
X_0(49)(Q) = {the two rational cusps}.
```

A rational point of exact order 49 generates a rational cyclic subgroup of order 49, hence a rational noncuspidal point of `Y_0(49)`. The fixed elliptic model is

```text
X_0(49) = 49a1:
  y^2 + xy = x^3 - x^2 - 2x - 1,
```

with rational group of order 2. Thus no noncuspidal rational point exists.

## Recommended formal route

There are two viable implementations.

### Explicit low-degree route

1. From the Tate point `P`, compute the rational subgroup `<7P>` of order 7.
2. Form the first rational 7-isogeny quotient by explicit Vélu formulas.
3. The image of `P` has order 7 on the quotient, giving a second rational 7-isogeny.
4. Normalize the two `X_0(7)` Hauptmoduli `s,t`.
5. Prove the 29-term non-backtracking equation `Q49(s,t)=0`.
6. Use the explicit birational map from this genus-one equation to `49a1`.
7. Prove the image is not a cusp using denominator/discriminant/lower-order factor certificates.

### Generated direct certificate

Generate rational functions `(X49,Y49,Z49)` on the primitive Tate component and prove the projective `49a1` identity modulo the primitive `prePsi'(49)` relation, together with exact cusp-preimage ideal-membership certificates.

The first route is conceptually cleaner and keeps individual formulas moderate. The second route is mechanically simple in Lean once the coefficient tables exist.

Then prove `49a1(Q)` has only two points by a fixed rational 2-isogeny descent and the same formal-kernel/reduction machinery used for N17 and N19.

## Infrastructure assessment

The arithmetic endpoint is only elliptic. The hard project-specific seam is the Tate/moduli map. A general theory of cyclic subgroup quotients or Vélu is absent from current Mathlib, but this can be bypassed with explicit formulas and ring certificates.

**Assessment:** known theorem; substantial but tractable fixed formalization; missing general isogeny/modular-map infrastructure, but no need for a high-dimensional Jacobian or deep new rank theorem.

# 7. `exists_rational_two_isogeny_quotient`

## Mathematical status

This is standard elementary elliptic-curve theory. A single rational nonzero point of order 2 is enough. Full rational 2-torsion is **not** required for the quotient construction; if the current axiom assumes full 2-torsion, that is a stronger hypothesis than necessary and simply supplies a choice of a rational kernel point.

Check the exact Lean signature before porting, because the name alone does not reveal whether it asserts:

- only existence of a target curve;
- an additive homomorphism with kernel of order 2;
- a dual map whose composites are `[2]`;
- or preservation/order information for a second rational point.

These variants have different implementation costs.

## Explicit construction

After an admissible change of variables put the curve in the form

```text
E : y^2 = x^3 + A x^2 + B x,
T = (0,0).
```

Then the standard quotient is

```text
E' : y^2 = x^3 - 2A x^2 + (A^2 - 4B)x.
```

Away from the kernel, one may use

```text
phi(x,y) = (y^2/x^2, y*(B-x^2)/x^2)
```

up to the project's sign convention, with the exceptional branches

```text
O |-> O,
T |-> O.
```

There is an explicit dual 2-isogeny, and the composites equal multiplication by 2. All landing identities are denominator-cleared polynomial identities. Additivity can be proved either from the explicit affine group law, from Vélu's formula, or by packaging the morphism into a small project-specific isogeny structure and proving the needed universal lemma once.

## Recommended Lean scope

Do **not** wait for a full scheme-theoretic quotient theory. Define only the interface consumed downstream, for example:

```lean
structure RationalTwoIsogenyData (E : WeierstrassCurve Q) where
  target : WeierstrassCurve Q
  phi    : E.toAffine.Point ->+ target.toAffine.Point
  dual   : target.toAffine.Point ->+ E.toAffine.Point
  phi_dual  : phi.comp dual = 2
  dual_phi  : dual.comp phi = 2
  kernel_phi : ...
```

Use the project's existing variable-change and affine-point formula lemmas. This becomes reusable in the rank-zero proofs for `17a1`, `19a2`, and `49a1`.

## Infrastructure assessment

Mathlib has Weierstrass curves, point groups, variable changes, and explicit group laws, but does not currently expose a turnkey Vélu/isogeny-quotient API matching this need. That is an infrastructure gap, not a mathematical difficulty.

**Assessment:** elementary known theorem; low mathematical risk; missing reusable isogeny infrastructure. This should be the first remaining axiom removed.

# Recommended shared implementation packages

The seven axioms should not be attacked as seven unrelated proofs. Four reusable packages cover most of the fixed cases.

## Package A: explicit degree-2 isogenies

Needed by:

```text
exists_rational_two_isogeny_quotient
X_0(17) rank-zero proof
X_0(19) rank-zero proof
X_0(49) rank-zero proof.
```

Deliverables:

```lean
RationalTwoIsogenyData
weakTwoDescent_of_kummer_certificate
formalKernel_separated_at_two
primeToTwo_reduction_injective
```

## Package B: fixed elliptic rational-point classification

A generic group-theoretic theorem should consume:

```text
weak descent modulo 2
+ finite exponent of the descent subgroup
+ reduced-group exponent
+ formal-kernel separatedness
```

and return a global annihilator. This is the pattern already used in N18. Instantiate it for 17, 19, and 49, then enumerate the finite torsion points.

## Package C: explicit modular/Tate bridge certificates

For each fixed level, separate:

```text
on-curve identity
all denominator nonvanishing
cusp-preimage factorization
exact-order/lower-order exclusions
orientation of source and target j-maps.
```

This keeps the large polynomial tables out of the conceptual theorem.

## Package D: rational generator fiber

For odd prime `p`, formalize once:

```text
rational exact-order-p point
  -> rational X_1(p) point
  -> rational point in the generator fiber over X_0(p)
  -> rational root of the representative cyclic-kernel polynomial.
```

Include the twist argument. Reuse it for N17 and N19.

# Practical priority and expected payoff

## Near-term

1. **`exists_rational_two_isogeny_quotient`**
   - smallest theorem;
   - unlocks all three elliptic rank-zero endpoints;
   - mostly ring identities and total-map branches.
2. **`no_order_19_prime`**
   - one noncuspidal fiber;
   - smallest fixed `X_0` group.
3. **`no_order_17_prime`**
   - same infrastructure, two fibers.
4. **`no_raw_order49_tate_obstruction`**
   - same rank-zero endpoint, larger bridge computation.
5. **`no_order_13_prime`**
   - exploit newly ported N18 genus-two infrastructure.

## Long-term

6. **`no_explicit_order25_obstruction`**
   - fixed but high-dimensional modular arithmetic.
7. **`no_prime_order_ge_23`**
   - uniform Mazur machinery and likely the final capstone.

A possible strategic alternative is to formalize the uniform Mazur/Eisenstein machinery before N25. A sufficiently strong uniform theorem may absorb parts of the order-25 argument, but it will not be a shorter near-term route; it simply moves the deepest infrastructure earlier.

- a stale import path selects the pre-port module.

# Bottom line

All seven remaining names describe true mathematics. They should not, however, be treated as seven equal “literature axioms.”

- `exists_rational_two_isogeny_quotient` is elementary and blocked mainly by missing API.
- N17, N19, and N49 are fixed elliptic arithmetic plus explicit modular bridges; they are realistic certificate projects.
- N13 is a serious genus-two descent, but the N18 port creates substantial reuse.
- N25 requires a high-dimensional modular-Jacobian rank/annihilator theorem.
- the prime tail requires the uniform heart of Mazur's argument and the largest missing infrastructure package.

The best engineering decision is to remove the first five with explicit fixed-level arithmetic while designing their common isogeny, reduction, and fiber APIs so that the eventual N25 and prime-tail work can reuse the same local and moduli interfaces.
