# Literature audit: how rational 18-torsion is actually excluded

## Executive verdict

The honest answer is:

1. **The classical global proof of Mazur's torsion theorem excludes order 18 without singling out a small Diophantine equation for `N=18`.** Kubert's 1976 work supplies Tate-normal-form calculations, modular-unit input, and the explicit universal families for the torsion groups that do occur; combined with Mazur's modular-curve/Eisenstein-ideal results, this yields the full classification. I did **not** find a Kubert lemma that reduces order 18 by itself to a Fermat-, Catalan-, or genus-1 equation.
2. **Every explicit determination of `X_1(18)(Q)` that I could locate goes through its genus-2 Jacobian**, either by accepting an earlier computation, by a genus-2 `2`-descent plus torsion enumeration, or by a Mazur-style reduction criterion applied to the Jacobian.
3. **I found no published rank-zero elliptic quotient route.** Modern sources treat `J_1(18)` as a two-dimensional abelian variety, not by mapping `X_1(18)` to a rank-zero elliptic curve. A natural order-3 automorphism has genus-zero quotient, not an elliptic quotient.
4. The best Lean target remains the fixed Jacobian route:

   ```text
   explicit X_1(18) model
       -> explicit 2-descent / weak descent on J
       -> 2-adic separatedness
       -> J(Q) killed by 21
       -> good-reduction torsion bound and 21-element Mumford table
       -> only six Abel-Jacobi images, all cusps.
   ```

   This is substantially more elementary than importing Mazur's full theorem, and it does not require a Cassels-Tate pairing if the `2`-Selmer upper bound is proved to be trivial by direct local squareclass checks. But it is still genus-2 Jacobian arithmetic.

The strongest negative conclusion justified by the search is therefore:

> No standalone published elementary descent for rational 18-torsion, analogous to the familiar factor descent for `X_1(16)`, was found. All explicit rational-point computations located for `X_1(18)` pass through `J_1(18)`.

This is a statement about the literature located, not a proof that an undiscovered elementary argument cannot exist.

# 1. The curve and the precise arithmetic statement

A standard literature model is

```text
C_lit : y^2 = F(x),
F(x) = x^6 + 2x^5 + 5x^4 + 10x^3 + 10x^2 + 4x + 1.
```

Kamienny-Najman identify this as `X_1(18)`. The model used in the current FLT project is the reciprocal sextic

```text
C : Y^2 = f(X),
f(X) = X^6 + 4X^5 + 10X^4 + 10X^3 + 5X^2 + 2X + 1.
```

They are isomorphic away from the points at zero and infinity by

```text
X = 1/x,
Y = y/x^3,
```

and the map extends to the smooth projective curves because

```text
f(X) = X^6 F(1/X).
```

The known rational points are exactly

```text
infinityPlus, infinityMinus,
(0,1), (0,-1),
(-1,1), (-1,-1),
```

and all six are cusps. Thus the explicit modular-curve statement is

```text
X_1(18)(Q) = {six cusps}.
```

The Jacobian data are

```text
rank J_1(18)(Q) = 0,
J_1(18)(Q)_tors ≅ Z/21Z.
```

The LMFDB genus-2 label is

```text
324.a.648.1,
```

and a current LMFDB data export records

```text
conductor 324,
Mordell-Weil rank 0,
torsion invariants [21].
```

The database entry is valuable confirmation, but it does not by itself identify which proof certified rank zero.

# 2. What the early literature did, and did not do

## 2.1 Beppo Levi: constructions and the conjectural list, not an `N=18` proof

The relevant historical work is **early twentieth century**, not nineteenth century. Between 1906 and 1911, Beppo Levi constructed families realizing the groups now on Mazur's list and proposed that the list was complete. His ICM communication is usually cited as

```text
B. Levi,
"Sull'equazione indeterminata del 3. ordine",
Atti del IV Congresso Internazionale dei Matematici,
Roma 1908, vol. II, published 1909, 175-177.
```

Levi's role was prophetic and constructive. I found no evidence that he gave a complete exclusion of rational order 18.

## 2.2 Ligozat: genus-one modular curves, not the genus-two `X_1(18)` computation

Ligozat's small-level modular-curve work is foundational, especially

```text
G. Ligozat,
Courbes modulaires de genre 1,
Bull. Soc. Math. France, Memoire 43 (1975).
```

Its natural domain is genus-one modular curves. `X_1(18)` is genus 2. The later sources inspected do not cite Ligozat for a determination of `X_1(18)(Q)` or for a special order-18 descent.

## 2.3 Mazur-Tate: the model case `N=13`, not `N=18`

Mazur and Tate's paper

```text
B. Mazur and J. Tate,
"Points of order 13 on elliptic curves",
Invent. Math. 22 (1973), 41-49
```

is the classical explicit genus-2 precedent. It proves rank zero for `J_1(13)` and then identifies the rational points. It should not be cited as an `N=18` computation.

Its methodological relevance is high: `X_1(13)` and `X_1(18)` both have genus 2, and the modern treatment of `X_1(18)` follows the same broad pattern—control the Jacobian, then inspect the finite torsion group.

## 2.4 Kubert 1976: the global composite-order complement, not a located standalone `18` descent

The exact reference is

```text
D. S. Kubert,
"Universal bounds on the torsion of elliptic curves",
Proc. London Math. Soc. (3) 33 (1976), 193-237.
```

Kubert's work is central for two reasons.

First, it systematizes the Tate normal form

```text
E(b,c): y^2 + (1-c)xy - by = x^3 - bx^2,
P=(0,0),
```

and the polynomial conditions expressing that `P` has prescribed order. Second, it gives the explicit universal families for the torsion structures that actually occur over `Q`. Modern surveys still describe the fifteen occurring rational torsion groups as having Kubert parametrizations.

For order 18, however, imposing the order condition does **not** produce a rational one-parameter family: it produces the positive-genus modular curve `X_1(18)`. The sources inspected use Kubert historically as the composite-order part of the global classification, but none points to a self-contained section of Kubert's paper proving

```text
X_1(18)(Q) = cusps
```

by an elementary factor descent.

A recent historical summary states that Mazur's prime-order results, together with Kubert's existing work, yielded the full rational torsion classification. That should be read as a statement about the **global proof architecture**, not as evidence that Kubert published a convenient Lean-sized `N=18` Diophantine contradiction.

I would therefore not cite “Kubert's elementary proof of the `N=18` case” unless an exact theorem/page is produced. I did not find such a theorem/page.

## 2.5 Mazur's full theorem

Mazur's modular-curve and Eisenstein-ideal work proves the complete rational torsion theorem. This is a valid proof of the nonexistence of rational 18-torsion, but it is not the kind of isolated elementary proof sought here. It brings in the geometry of modular curves, reduction, formal immersion, component groups, and Eisenstein ideals.

Thus there are two very different statements:

```text
A. Mazur's theorem implies there is no rational 18-torsion.
B. There is a short standalone elementary descent for X_1(18).
```

`A` is classical and established. The literature search did not find `B`.

## 2.6 Kenku-Momose and Reichert are quadratic-field literature

Kenku-Momose classify possible torsion over quadratic fields. Their role is not to provide the original proof over `Q` of the order-18 exclusion.

Reichert's paper

```text
M. A. Reichert,
"Explicit determination of nontrivial torsion structures of
elliptic curves over quadratic number fields",
Math. Comp. 46 (1986), 637-658
```

constructs explicit examples over quadratic fields. In particular, the standard later citation uses Reichert's example over

```text
Q(sqrt(33))
```

for an elliptic curve with a point of order 18. Reichert is therefore evidence for **existence over a quadratic field**, not a proof of nonexistence over `Q`.

## 2.7 “Blass”

I did not locate a reliable bibliographic item by Blass that contains an `N=18` proof. The name may refer to a different part of the early torsion literature or may be a citation-memory mismatch. It should not be used in the formalization bibliography without an exact title and theorem.

# 3. The first explicit modern statements about `X_1(18)(Q)`

## 3.1 Poonen's paper states the result but attributes it privately

Poonen uses the model

```text
C : y^2 = x^6 + 2x^5 + 5x^4 + 10x^3 + 10x^2 + 4x + 1
```

and states that it is `X_1(18)` and has exactly six rational points, all cusps. But the supporting reference in that paper is an electronic communication from Noam Elkies in June 1995.

Reference:

```text
B. Poonen,
"The classification of rational preperiodic points of quadratic
polynomials over Q: a refined conjecture",
Math. Z. 228 (1998), 11-29;
arXiv:math/9512217.
```

This is important negative evidence: Poonen is often cited for the six-point fact, but his paper is not a printed derivation of the rank-zero calculation for `J_1(18)`. His detailed `2`-descent later in the paper concerns another, nonmodular genus-2 curve.

## 3.2 Kamienny-Najman give the clearest published method description

Kamienny-Najman list the model and explain the computational method explicitly:

```text
S. Kamienny and F. Najman,
"Torsion groups of elliptic curves over quadratic fields",
Acta Arith. 152 (2012), 291-305;
arXiv:1103.5906.
```

Their Section 2 says:

- genus-one modular curves are handled by ordinary `2`-descent on the elliptic curve;
- hyperelliptic `X_1(13)` and `X_1(18)` are handled by computing the rank of the genus-2 Jacobian using `2`-descent;
- if the Jacobian rank is zero, determine its torsion and test which torsion classes come from points on the curve;
- generic genus-2 `2`-descent may return only an upper bound, so they also formulate a Mazur-style reduction criterion for rank zero;
- `X_1(16)` is specifically contrasted with `X_1(18)`: its defining polynomial is reducible, allowing a cover by two elliptic curves and hence more elementary methods.

That contrast is particularly decisive for the present question. The authors explicitly single out `X_1(16)`, not `X_1(18)`, as the factorable/easier genus-2 case.

For order 18 over quadratic fields, their Theorem 8 computes

```text
rank J_1(18)(Q(sqrt(d))) = 0,
J_1(18)(Q(sqrt(d))) = Z/21Z
```

for the residual list of small fields, then concludes there are no noncuspidal points. The rational case is the base torsion group appearing in this calculation.

## 3.3 Recent computations continue to use two-cover descent and the Jacobian

Banwait-Derickx's work on quadratic torsion uses the same model and, for twists of `X_1(18)`, runs Magma's `TwoCoverDescent`, point searches, and in unresolved cases a Mordell-Weil sieve.

Reference:

```text
B. S. Banwait and M. Derickx,
"Torsion subgroups of elliptic curves over quadratic fields and
a conjecture of Granville",
arXiv:2401.14514.
```

Their public code states directly that the `X_1(13)` and `X_1(18)` calculations use two-descent and point search; remaining twist cases use a Mordell-Weil sieve. This is modern corroboration that no standard factor shortcut for `X_1(18)` has replaced the Jacobian computation.

# 4. Is there an explicit rank-zero elliptic quotient?

## Answer: none was found, and the known symmetry does not provide one

A nonconstant map

```text
X_1(18) -> E
```

to an elliptic curve would induce a nonzero elliptic quotient of `J_1(18)`. Such a map would be an excellent elementary shortcut: determine `E(Q)`, then analyze the finite fibers.

The literature located does not use such a map. Instead:

- Kamienny-Najman work directly with `J_1(18)`;
- Poonen imports the complete point result;
- modern twist computations use genus-2 two-cover descent;
- the LMFDB records a dimension-2 isogeny class `324.a`, not a decomposition into named elliptic factors in the data inspected.

There is an order-3 automorphism used in modern work. Its quotient has genus 0. That is useful for organizing twists and rational fibers, but it is **not** an elliptic quotient and it does not prove that the covering curve has only cusps over `Q`.

I did not independently verify from the endomorphism database page the strongest formulation

```text
J_1(18) is Q-simple.
```

The available evidence is consistent with it, and no elliptic factor appears in the sources inspected, but a Lean development does not need this negative statement. It is enough to record the practical literature fact:

> No published `X_1(18) -> E` rank-zero elliptic quotient proof was found.

If one wishes to assert `Q`-simplicity formally, the clean route is to identify the weight-2 newform orbit attached to `J_1(18)` and prove that its coefficient field has degree 2, so the associated modular abelian variety is a simple surface rather than a product of rational-newform elliptic curves.

# 5. What the LMFDB data do and do not establish

The current genus-2 database record is

```text
324.a.648.1.
```

A public LMFDB data export gives a row with

```text
label          324.a.648.1
isogeny class  324.a
conductor      324
rank           0
torsion        [21]
```

This agrees perfectly with the modular-curve calculation.

However, an LMFDB rank field is not a bibliographic proof certificate. Depending on the record, rank data may have been obtained or cross-checked by combinations of:

- explicit `2`-descent;
- analytic rank and modularity;
- generators and saturation;
- reduction computations;
- database imports from Magma/Sage computations.

For a Lean proof, one should not use “LMFDB says rank zero” as the theorem. One should extract a finite certificate.

For this particular curve, a direct genus-2 `2`-descent can prove a trivial `2`-Selmer upper bound. If the local squareclass calculation shows that the only everywhere locally admissible class is the identity, then

```text
J(Q)/2J(Q) = 0
```

follows without any Cassels-Tate pairing. Combining this weak descent with `2`-adic separatedness gives the finite-exponent conclusion, exactly as in the audited group-theory lemma from Q4293.

Thus the method is elementary in the arithmetic-geometry sense:

```text
number-field arithmetic + S-units + local squareclasses
+ polynomial Jacobian arithmetic + p-adic separatedness.
```

But published computational papers generally call a Magma routine and do not print the complete local matrix and obstruction table needed by Lean. That missing finite certificate is the actual formalization bottleneck, not a conceptual use of Cassels-Tate.

# 6. Does the Kubert order-18 equation factor or reduce further?

## 6.1 Tate-normal-form structure

Starting from

```text
E(b,c): y^2 + (1-c)xy - by = x^3 - bx^2,
P=(0,0),
```

one may express the coordinates of `nP` recursively and impose `18P=O`. Equivalently, since a point of order 18 gives a point of order 9 and a rational point of order 2, one can:

1. use the genus-zero family for a marked point of order 9;
2. require the `2`-division cubic to have a rational root;
3. eliminate the auxiliary coordinates.

The resulting curve is birational to the genus-2 `X_1(18)` model above.

This is an explicit construction, but it is not yet a proof of nonexistence. The order condition has encoded the difficult rational-point problem rather than solved it.

## 6.2 No `Q`-factor descent analogous to `X_1(16)`

The sextic defining `X_1(18)` is irreducible over `Q`. Kamienny-Najman explicitly contrast this with `X_1(16)`, whose polynomial factors and permits a cover by two elliptic curves.

So the standard pattern

```text
sextic = lower-degree factor * lower-degree factor
-> squareclass split
-> two elliptic curves
-> elementary finite check
```

does not apply over `Q` to the displayed `X_1(18)` equation.

The fixed curve has additional arithmetic structure. For the reciprocal FLT model,

```text
q = x^3 + 2x^2 + 3x - 1,
f = q^2 + 8x,
```

and the degree-six field contains `Q(sqrt(-2))`. These facts make a hand-built `2`-descent unusually tractable. But I found no published paper turning this identity into a standalone Fermat/Catalan contradiction or into an elliptic covering proof of all rational points.

## 6.3 The order-3 quotient is genus zero, not a solution

An order-3 diamond/automorphism quotient gives a degree-3 map to a genus-zero curve. A rational point on `X_1(18)` therefore yields a rational point on `P^1`, but the converse is controlled by a cubic fiber. Proving that only the cusp fibers contain rational points is another Diophantine problem. The quotient organizes the calculation; it does not remove it.

# 7. The most elementary published route that can actually be followed

The most explicit route supported by the published literature is:

## Step 1: derive the genus-2 modular curve

Use Tate normal form/Kubert division calculations to derive

```text
y^2 = x^6 + 2x^5 + 5x^4 + 10x^3 + 10x^2 + 4x + 1.
```

Identify the cusp locus. Over `Q`, the six rational cusp points are the two infinities and the points above `x=0,-1`.

## Step 2: prove weak `2`-descent on the Jacobian

Use the etale algebra

```text
L = Q[T]/(f)
```

and the `x-T` map into the appropriate even-degree full/fake `2`-descent target. Show by S-unit and local calculations at the bad places that the full `2`-Selmer upper bound is trivial.

For this sextic, the relevant bad rational primes are `2` and `3`. The number field has enough explicit structure that the global squareclass space is finite and small.

The desired output is

```text
for every D in J(Q), there exists E in J(Q) with D = 2E,
```

or, more generally, the weak-descent decomposition by a known finite subgroup.

## Step 3: replace Mordell-Weil finite generation by separatedness

Rather than invoke Mordell-Weil plus the structure theorem, prove directly that no nonzero element of `J(Q)` is infinitely divisible by 2. The fixed `2`-adic formal-neighborhood argument then combines with weak descent to show that `J(Q)` has the exponent of the known torsion subgroup.

This is the reusable abstract argument audited in Q4293.

## Step 4: determine the torsion subgroup

Exhibit the cusp divisor

```text
delta = infinityPlus - infinityMinus
```

and verify by explicit Mumford arithmetic that it has order 21. Use reduction at good primes to bound rational torsion by 21, or combine the weak descent/exponent result with a finite reduction injection.

Conclude

```text
J(Q) ≅ Z/21Z.
```

## Step 5: inspect Abel-Jacobi images

Enumerate the 21 reduced balanced Mumford representatives. Check which have the form

```text
[P - infinityMinus]
```

for a rational point `P` on the curve. Exactly six do, and they are the cusps.

This final step is finite polynomial arithmetic.

## Lean assessment

This route is formalizable, and it avoids the full machinery of Mazur's theorem. Its major pieces are:

```text
1. fixed genus-2 Mumford composition/reduction;
2. fixed sextic number-field arithmetic;
3. the local 2-descent image at infinity, 2, and 3;
4. fixed 2-adic separatedness;
5. finite torsion and Abel-Jacobi tables.
```

The only currently missing numerical ingredient from the Q4284 computation is the complete local Kummer-image matrix/obstruction table. Once that is obtained, the rest is certificate checking rather than open-ended Diophantine search.

# 8. Direct answers to the four questions

## (1) How did the classical literature handle `N=18` specifically?

- Beppo Levi constructed the expected families and conjectured completeness; he did not supply a complete order-18 exclusion found in this search.
- Ligozat handled important genus-one modular-curve cases; `X_1(18)` is genus 2, and no Ligozat-specific `N=18` proof was found.
- Mazur-Tate explicitly handled `N=13`, not `18`.
- Kubert provided the Tate-normal-form and composite-order infrastructure that, combined with Mazur's work, yields the full classification. No separately identifiable Kubert elementary `N=18` descent was found.
- Kenku-Momose concerns quadratic fields and is not the original rational proof.
- Reichert supplies an order-18 example over `Q(sqrt(33))`, not an exclusion over `Q`.
- Poonen states the six-point result but cites private communication from Elkies.
- The clearest published explicit method description is Kamienny-Najman: genus-2 Jacobian rank zero, torsion `Z/21`, then cusp inspection.

## (2) Was `X_1(18)` handled by an explicit rank-zero elliptic quotient?

No such published route was found. The known order-3 quotient is genus zero. The modern literature works directly with the abelian-surface Jacobian.

## (3) Is the Jacobian rank-zero proof elementary enough for Lean?

Yes in principle, via an explicit genus-2 `2`-descent with trivial local Selmer set. Such a proof need not use Cassels-Tate. But the LMFDB entry and papers invoking Magma do not print all the finite local certificate data. Lean still needs those matrices/witnesses explicitly.

Kamienny-Najman also give a Mazur-style reduction criterion because generic `2`-descent software may return only an upper bound. That alternative still concerns the genus-2 Jacobian; it is not an elliptic quotient shortcut.

## (4) Does Kubert's order-18 curve have a known factorization making finiteness elementary?

Not over `Q` in the sense needed. The resulting sextic is irreducible, unlike the `X_1(16)` sextic highlighted in the literature. It has useful structure over `Q(sqrt(-2))` and an order-3 symmetry, but no published reduction to a standard impossible equation was found.

Therefore the most elementary published explicit route remains the Jacobian route.
