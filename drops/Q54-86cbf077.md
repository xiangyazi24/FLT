# N = 49: the primitive Tate curve, the map to `49a1`, and a Lean-checkable proof

## Executive answer

The shortest sound route is

```text
exact order-49 Tate witness (b,c)
  -> two consecutive, non-backtracking 7-isogenies
  -> an affine point (S,T) on a genus-one model Q49 of X_0(49)
  -> an explicit rational point (X,Y) on 49a1
  -> X_0(49)(Q) = {O,(2,-1)}
  -> both points are cusps
  -> contradiction, because the Tate witness is noncuspidal.
```

The four answers are:

1. **Irreducibility and genus.** The primitive exact-order component is a geometrically irreducible curve birational to `X_1(49)`, and its smooth projective normalization has
   \[
   \boxed{g(X_1(49))=69.}
   \]
   This is the answer for the saturated primitive component. I would not assert that the literal computer-generated quotient called `G` is irreducible until its content, repeated factors, denominator/cusp factors, and the factor `b^784` have all been removed. The degree `157` of a plane equation is a coordinate artifact and is not its geometric genus.

2. **Explicit map.** There is a practical explicit map. Do not expand it into one enormous pair of polynomials in `b,c`. Compute the first degree-7 Vélu quotient, evaluate the image of `P`, form two explicit `X_0(7)` Hauptmodul values `S,T`, and apply the displayed rational formulas
   \[
   (S,T)\longmapsto (X,Y)\in 49\mathrm a1.
   \]
   Every operation is a rational function and is suitable for exact polynomial certificates in Lean.

3. **Kenku.** Kenku's 1981 paper is titled *On the Modular Curves \(X_0(125),X_1(25),X_1(49)\)*, not “a paper proving the rational points of `X_0(49)`” as its principal statement. The safe historical claim is that the `X_1(49)` torsion exclusion uses the modular quotient and the arithmetic of the genus-one quotient. I could not inspect the paywalled body closely enough to claim that Kenku used the exact binary quartics below. The modern Lean proof can and should be self-contained: identify `X_0(49)` with `49a1`, then perform an elementary 2-isogeny descent.

4. **Lean certificate.** Yes. After the change
   \[
   U=4(x-2),\qquad V=8y+4x,
   \]
   the curve becomes
   \[
   V^2=U^3+21U^2+112U.
   \]
   Its 2-isogenous curve is
   \[
   Z^2=X^3-42X^2-7X.
   \]
   Eight finite local non-solubility checks, six modulo `16` or `49`, reduce the two Kummer images to two squareclasses each. They can be closed by `native_decide`. The resulting weak descent says every rational point is a two-torsion point plus twice another point. A short 2-adic formal-kernel separatedness argument then proves that the only rational points are `O` and `(2,-1)` without needing a formalized Mordell--Weil finite-generation theorem.

# 1. What the degree-157 polynomial actually represents

Let

\[
W_{b,c}: y^2+(1-c)xy-by=x^3-bx^2,
\qquad P=(0,0).
\]

The question uses the sign convention with `a3 = -b`. If a file writes `+ b y`, change the signs consistently before comparing formulas.

For an elliptic Tate-normal-form curve, the conditions

\[
\operatorname{pre\Psi}_{49}(0)=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{pre\Psi}_{7}(0)\ne0,
\qquad b\ne0
\]

say that `P` has exact order `49`, once the division-polynomial evaluation theorem has been connected to scalar multiplication.

The open moduli problem

\[
(E,P),\qquad P\text{ of exact order }49,
\]

is `Y_1(49)`. Tate normal form is a birational affine chart of this moduli curve. Consequently the **primitive saturated component** of the division-polynomial equation is geometrically irreducible.

There are three different objects that must not be conflated:

```text
raw prePsi_49(0),
raw quotient prePsi_49(0) / prePsi_7(0),
primitive saturated exact-order factor defining the Tate chart of Y_1(49).
```

The raw quotient may still contain:

- powers of `b` coming from the origin normalization;
- repeated factors;
- factors supported on singular Tate curves;
- lower-order or denominator components introduced by clearing fractions;
- cusp components in the affine closure.

Thus the precise statement is:

> After taking primitive content, squarefree part, and saturation by the Tate discriminant and all chart denominators, the unique exact-order component is absolutely irreducible.

For the literal current `G`, run an exact factorization. A suitable Magma check is conceptually:

```magma
Q := Rationals();
A2<b,c> := AffineSpace(Q,2);

G0 := /* quotient after the exact divisions */;
G0 := SquarefreePart(G0 div b^784);

Factorization(G0);
C := Curve(A2,G0);
IsGeometricallyIrreducible(C);

Cbar := ProjectiveClosure(C);
Ctilde := Normalization(Cbar);

```

If `Factorization(G0)` has more than one factor, select the factor surviving saturation by

```text
b * c * TateDiscriminant * prePsi_7 * all map denominators.
```

No part of the final exclusion proof needs a Lean theorem saying that this degree-157 polynomial is irreducible.

## 1.1 Genus computation

For `N=49`, the index in `PSL_2(Z)` is

\[
\mu
 =\frac{N^2}{2}\prod_{p\mid N}\left(1-\frac1{p^2}\right)
 =\frac{49^2}{2}\left(1-\frac1{7^2}\right)
 =1176.
\]

There are no elliptic fixed points for `Gamma_1(49)`. The number of cusps is

\[
\frac12\sum_{d\mid49}\varphi(d)\varphi(49/d)
 =\frac12(42+36+42)=60.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
 g(X_1(49))
 =1+\frac{1176}{12}-\frac{60}{2}
 =69.
}
\]

The forgetful map

\[
X_1(49)\longrightarrow X_0(49),
\qquad (E,P)\longmapsto(E,\langle P\rangle)
\]

has degree

\[
\frac{\varphi(49)}{2}=21.
\]

The target `X_0(49)` has genus `1`.

If `157` is the degree in one variable or an effective elimination degree, it says nothing directly about the genus. Even if it were the total degree of a plane closure, the plane arithmetic genus would be enormous and the difference from `69` would be accounted for by singularities at cusps and infinity.

## 1.2 Why the finite-field experiment is expected to fail

A geometrically irreducible positive-genus curve normally has points over many finite fields. The assertion needed is “all rational points are cusps,” not “the curve has no points modulo some prime.”

Moreover, even absence of affine points with `b != 0` modulo `p` does not automatically exclude a rational point: after choosing integral coordinates, that point can reduce to a cusp, a denominator component, or `b=0`. A modular reduction proof would require a proper model and a theorem controlling reduction of every exact-order rational point into the tested affine open.

# 2. A compositional explicit map from the Tate chart to `49a1`

The map below is explicit but deliberately factored into small rational operations. This is far better for Lean than one expanded numerator with millions of terms.

## 2.1 Small Tate factors and multiples of `P`

Put

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_5&=b-c,\\
F_6&=b-c-c^2,\\
F_7&=b^2-bc-c^3,\\
F_8&=2b^2-bc^2-3bc+c^2,\\
F_9&=b^3-3b^2c+bc^3+3bc^2-c^5-c^4-c^3.
\end{aligned}
\]

Starting at

\[
3P=(c,b-c),
\]

addition by `P` is

\[
\boxed{
 x((n+1)P)=\frac{b\,y(nP)}{x(nP)^2},
 \qquad
 y((n+1)P)=\frac{b^2(x(nP)^2-y(nP))}{x(nP)^3}.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

For example,

\[
\begin{aligned}
x(4P)&=\frac{bF_5}{c^2},
&y(4P)&=-\frac{b^2F_6}{c^3},\\
x(5P)&=-\frac{bcF_6}{F_5^2},
&y(5P)&=\frac{bc^2F_7}{F_5^3},\\
x(6P)&=\frac{F_5F_7}{F_6^2},
&y(6P)&=-\frac{cF_5^2F_8}{F_6^3},\\
x(7P)&=\frac{bcF_6F_8}{F_7^2},
&y(7P)&=\frac{b^2F_6^2F_9}{F_7^3}.
\end{aligned}
\]

These signs use `F7 = b^2-bc-c^3`. A source using `c^3-b^2+bc` has the opposite signs in the odd powers.

Use (2.1), or the existing projective scalar-multiplication formulas, to define

\[
Q_1=7P,\qquad Q_2=14P,\qquad Q_3=21P.
\]

The six nonzero points of the first kernel are

\[
\{\pm Q_1,\pm Q_2,\pm Q_3\}=\langle 7P\rangle\setminus\{0\}.
\]

## 2.2 The degree-7 Vélu formulas on a long Weierstrass equation

For

\[
E:y^2+a_1xy+a_3y=x^3+a_2x^2+a_4x+a_6
\]

and a kernel representative `Q=(q_x,q_y)`, define

\[
\begin{aligned}
g_x(Q)&=(3q_x+2a_2)q_x+a_4-a_1q_y,\\
g_y(Q)&=-2q_y-a_1q_x-a_3,\\
u(Q)&=g_y(Q)^2,\\
v(Q)&=2g_x(Q)-a_1g_y(Q).
\end{aligned}
\]

For a variable point `(x,y)` and `delta=x-q_x`, put

\[
\begin{aligned}
K_x(Q;x)
 &=\frac{v(Q)}{\delta}+\frac{u(Q)}{\delta^2},\\
K_y(Q;x,y)
 &=\frac{u(Q)(2y+a_1x+a_3)}{\delta^3}\\
 &\quad+\frac{v(Q)(a_1\delta+y-q_y)+a_1u(Q)-g_x(Q)g_y(Q)}{\delta^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Then the first isogeny is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\phi_1^x(x,y)&=x+\sum_{i=1}^3K_x(Q_i;x),\\
\phi_1^y(x,y)&=y-\sum_{i=1}^3K_y(Q_i;x,y).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Let

\[
V_0=\sum_{i=1}^3v(Q_i),
\qquad
W_0=\sum_{i=1}^3\bigl(u(Q_i)+q_{i,x}v(Q_i)\bigr).
\]

The quotient curve `E1=E/<7P>` has

\[
\boxed{
 a_1'=a_1,\quad a_2'=a_2,\quad a_3'=a_3,
 \quad a_4'=a_4-5V_0,
 \quad a_6'=a_6-(a_1^2+4a_2)V_0-7W_0.
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

For the Tate curve,

```text
a1 = 1-c,
a2 = -b,
a3 = -b,
a4 = 0,
a6 = 0.
```

Set

\[
R=\phi_1(P).
\]

Because `P` has order `49` and `ker(phi1)=<7P>`, the point `R` has exact order `7` on `E1`.

All formulas in (2.1)--(2.3) are rational functions in `b,c`.

## 2.3 Normalize an order-7 pair and extract the `X_0(7)` coordinate

For a long Weierstrass curve with coefficients `a1,...,a4` and a marked affine point `(r,w)`, define

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_3&=a_3+r a_1+2w,\\
\lambda&=\frac{3r^2+2a_2r+a_4-a_1w}{D_3},\\
A_1&=a_1+2\lambda,\\
A_2&=a_2-\lambda a_1+3r-\lambda^2,\\
A_3&=D_3,\\
d&=-\frac{A_2^3}{A_3(A_3-A_1A_2)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.4}
\]

This `d` is the `b/c` parameter of the Tate-normalized order-7 pair. Define the `q^{-1}`-normalized Hauptmodul

\[
\boxed{
T_7(d)=\frac{49d(d-1)}{d^3-8d^2+5d+1}.
}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Now form

\[
\begin{aligned}
S&=T_7\bigl(d(E,Q_1)\bigr),\\
T&=T_7\bigl(d(E_1,R)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.6}
\]

The first quantity records the first 7-isogeny and the second records the second 7-isogeny.

## 2.6 Cusp exclusion is elementary in these coordinates

The finite cusp `(2,-1)` has a particularly simple preimage.

If `(X,Y)=(2,-1)`, then (2.9) gives `d=0`. At `d=0`,

\[
Y=\frac{T-7}{7},
\]

so `Y=-1` gives `T=0`. But

\[
q_{49}(S,0)=-49^6S,
\]

hence `S=0`.

Thus

\[
\boxed{(X,Y)=(2,-1)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad(S,T)=(0,0).}
\tag{2.14}
\]

For an exact noncuspidal 7-isogeny, the normalized `d` is neither `0` nor `1`; therefore `T7(d) != 0`. Prove this for both stages from the nonvanishing Tate discriminant and exact-order assumptions. Then `S != 0` and `T != 0`, so (2.14) excludes the finite cusp.

The other rational cusp is the point at infinity. The constructed point is affine because all denominators in (2.9) are nonzero, so it cannot be infinity.

This completely avoids an abstract moduli-space proof of noncuspidality.

The historically and mathematically safe summary is:

1. Pass from a point of order `49` to a cyclic subgroup of order `49`.

# 4. A completely explicit 2-isogeny descent on `49a1`

Let

\[
E_{49}: y^2+xy=x^3-x^2-2x-1.
\]

Its rational point

\[
T=(2,-1)
\]

has order `2`.

## 4.1 Split model

Set

\[
\boxed{U=4(x-2),\qquad V=8y+4x.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The inverse change is

\[
\boxed{x=\frac U4+2,\qquad y=\frac{V-U-8}{8}.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

A direct expansion gives

\[
\boxed{E:V^2=U^3+21U^2+112U.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

The two-torsion point is now `(0,0)`.

The 2-isogenous curve is

\[
\boxed{\widehat E:Z^2=X^3-42X^2-7X.}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Away from the kernel points, the isogenies are

\[
\boxed{
\phi(U,V)=
 \left(\frac{V^2}{U^2},\frac{V(112-U^2)}{U^2}\right),
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\widehat\phi(X,Z)=
 \left(\frac{Z^2}{4X^2},
       \frac{Z(-7-X^2)}{8X^2}\right).
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

The exceptional clauses are

```text
phi(O)=O,          phi(0,0)=O,
phihat(O)=O,       phihat(0,0)=O.
```

Direct rational identities prove

\[
\widehat\phi\circ\phi=[2]_E,
\qquad
\phi\circ\widehat\phi=[2]_{\widehat E}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Each landing and composition proof is `field_simp` plus `ring`, split off at `U=0` or `X=0`.

## 4.2 Kummer covers

For

\[
C_{A,B}: y^2=x^3+Ax^2+Bx
\]

with rational point `(0,0)`, define the Kummer map

\[
\alpha(O)=1,
\qquad
\alpha(0,0)=B,
\qquad
\alpha(x,y)=x
\quad\text{in }\mathbf Q^\times/(\mathbf Q^\times)^2.
\]

A squareclass `d`, represented by a squarefree divisor of `B`, lies in the image only if the binary quartic

\[
\boxed{
w^2=d u^4+A u^2v^2+\frac Bd v^4
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

has a primitive rational, equivalently primitive integral, solution.

Equivalently, without dividing by `d`, use

\[
\boxed{
dw^2=d^2u^4+Ad u^2v^2+Bv^4.}
\tag{4.9}
\]

The exactness theorem is

\[
\ker\alpha_E=\widehat\phi(\widehat E(\mathbf Q)),
\qquad
\ker\alpha_{\widehat E}=\phi(E(\mathbf Q)).
\tag{4.10}
\]

This can be proved with explicit preimage formulas. For example, if `x(P)=r^2 != 0`, then one preimage under the dual map has

\[
X_Q=A+2r^2+\frac{2y(P)}r,
\qquad
Z_Q=2rX_Q.
\]

The other exceptional cases are finite.

## 4.3 Candidate classes for `E`

For (4.3),

\[
A=21,\qquad B=112.
\]

The candidate squareclasses are

\[
\{\pm1,\pm2,\pm7,\pm14\}.
\]

The following six branches are locally impossible:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
d&\text{quartic }w^2=&\text{check}\\ \hline
 2&2u^4+21u^2v^2+56v^4&\bmod 16\\
14&14u^4+21u^2v^2+8v^4&\bmod 16\\
-1&-u^4+21u^2v^2-112v^4&\bmod 49\\
-2&-2u^4+21u^2v^2-56v^4&\bmod 49\\
-7&-7u^4+21u^2v^2-16v^4&\bmod 49\\
-14&-14u^4+21u^2v^2-8v^4&\bmod 49.
\end{array}
\tag{4.11}
\]

For the modulo-16 checks, require that `u,v` are not both even. For modulo-49 checks, require that `u,v` are not both divisible by `7`.

The surviving classes are

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{im}\alpha_E=\{1,7\}.}
\tag{4.12}
\]

The class `1` is represented by the identity and class `7` by `(0,0)`, since `112` has squareclass `7`.

## 4.4 Candidate classes for the dual curve

For (4.4),

\[
A'=-42,\qquad B'=-7.
\]

The candidates are `±1,±7`. Eliminate

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
d&\text{quartic }w^2=&\text{check}\\ \hline
-1&-u^4-42u^2v^2+7v^4&\bmod16,\\
 7&7u^4-42u^2v^2-v^4&\bmod16.
\end{array}
\tag{4.13}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{im}\alpha_{\widehat E}=\{1,-7\}.}
\tag{4.14}
\]

Again `-7` is represented by the dual kernel point `(0,0)`.

## 4.5 Lean `native_decide` form

A reusable finite checker is:

```lean
def primitiveAt (p : Nat) (u v : Nat) : Bool :=
  !((u % p == 0) && (v % p == 0))

def quarticExpr (A B d : Int) (u v w : Nat) : Int :=
  d * (w : Int)^2 -
    (d^2 * (u : Int)^4 + A*d*(u : Int)^2*(v : Int)^2 + B*(v : Int)^4)

def noPrimitiveSolutionsMod
    (N p : Nat) (A B d : Int) : Bool :=
  (List.range N).all fun u =>
  (List.range N).all fun v =>
    !primitiveAt p u v ||
      (List.range N).all fun w => quarticExpr A B d u v w % N != 0
```

Then the eight statements corresponding to (4.11) and (4.13) close with

```lean
by native_decide
```

The bridge from a rational quartic point to a primitive residue solution is ordinary denominator clearing plus division by `gcd(u,v)`.

## 4.6 Weak descent, without invoking a rank theorem

Equations (4.12) and (4.10) imply that every `P in E(Q)` has the form

\[
P=\epsilon T+\widehat\phi(R),
\qquad \epsilon\in\{0,1\},\quad R\in\widehat E(\mathbf Q).
\]

Equations (4.14) and (4.10) imply

\[
R=\epsilon'\widehat T+\phi(Q).
\]

Because `phihat(widehat T)=0`, equations (4.7) give

\[
\boxed{
\forall P\in E(\mathbf Q),\quad
\exists\epsilon\in\{0,1\},\ \exists Q\in E(\mathbf Q),\quad
P=\epsilon T+2Q.
}
\tag{4.15}
\]

This is the best theorem for the Lean critical path. It contains the rank-zero content needed downstream but does not require Mathlib to know that `E(Q)` is finitely generated.

## 4.7 Finish by 2-adic separatedness

Iterate (4.15). If

\[
P=\epsilon_0T+2Q_0,
\]

then write `Q0=epsilon1*T+2Q1`. Since `2T=0`,

\[
P-\epsilon_0T=4Q_1.
\]

Continuing shows

\[
P-\epsilon_0T\in\bigcap_{n\ge1}2^nE(\mathbf Q).
\tag{4.16}
\]

The discriminant of `49a1` is `-343`, so the curve has good reduction at `2`. Its reduced curve has exactly two points:

\[
\#E(\mathbf F_2)=2.
\]

Hence the double of every reduced point is zero. If `R=2^nQ_n`, then `2Q_n` lies in the kernel of reduction and

\[
R=2^{n-1}(2Q_n).
\]

Thus a point in (4.16) is infinitely 2-divisible **inside the formal kernel**.

For the local parameter `z=-x/y`, the integral formal group has

\[
[2](z)=2z+z^2A(z),\qquad A(z)\in\mathbf Z_2[[z]].
\]

If `r=v_2(z)>=1`, then

\[
v_2([2]z)\ge\min(r+1,2r)\ge r+1.
\tag{4.17}
\]

Repeated doubling therefore raises the formal depth. A fixed nonzero point cannot lie at every depth. Consequently

\[
\bigcap_{n\ge1}2^nE(\mathbf Q)\cap\ker(\mathrm{red}_2)=\{0\}.
\]

From (4.16),

\[
P=\epsilon_0T.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{E_{49}(\mathbf Q)=\{O,(2,-1)\}\cong\mathbf Z/2\mathbf Z.}
\tag{4.18}
\]

Under the fixed modular identification, these are the cusps `infinity` and `0`.

## 4.8 Optional finite-reduction torsion check

As an independent check,

\[
\#E(\mathbf F_2)=2,
\qquad
\#E(\mathbf F_5)=6.
\]

The affine points over `F_2` are just `(0,1)`. Over `F_5`, the affine points are

```text
(0,2), (0,3), (1,1), (1,3), (2,4).
```

Prime-to-`p` torsion injects under good reduction. Thus odd torsion is killed by the count at `2`, while the 2-primary torsion has order dividing the 2-part of `6`, namely `2`. Since `(2,-1)` exists, the torsion subgroup is exactly `Z/2`.
