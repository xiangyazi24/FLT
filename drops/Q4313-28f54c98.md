# \(N=35\): concrete \(X_1(35)	o X_0(35)	o 35\mathrm a1\) reduction

## Executive verdict

There are two distinct issues, and they should not be conflated.

1. **The arithmetic endpoint is genuinely small and elementary.**  A standard model of \(X_0(35)\) has a degree-two Atkin--Lehner quotient birational to the rank-zero elliptic curve \(35\mathrm a1\).  The quotient map, the three rational points of \(35\mathrm a1\), and every fiber above those points admit short explicit formulas.  No genus-\(3\) Jacobian descent is needed.

2. **The raw Kubert-to-\(X_0(35)\) coordinate bridge is the bulky part.**  Starting from
   
   \[
   G_{35}(t,X)=0,\qquad V^2=D_2(t,X),
   \]
   
   one must quotient by the degree-\(12\) diamond action and normalize before reaching a convenient genus-\(3\) model.  I did not find a published coefficient list for that rational map in Kubert coordinates.  It should be generated once by Magma/Sage and then checked by polynomial identities.  This is an **explicit-map wall, not a Diophantine wall**.

Thus:

- with the modular interpretation \( (E,P_{35})\mapsto(E,\langle P_{35}angle)\) available as an abstract theorem, \(N=35\) is close to \(N=21\) and is a days-scale arithmetic proof;
- if the proof must begin with the raw \((t,X,V)\) equations and end with one giant `ring` certificate, the map-generation phase is substantially larger than \(N=21\), although the generated certificate is completely finite and checkable.

The useful concrete chain is

\[
(t,X,V)\dashrightarrow (z,w)\in X_0(35)\dashrightarrow (u,v)\in C
\dashrightarrow (x,y)\in 35\mathrm a1,
\]

where the last two arrows are given explicitly below, together with compact certifying identities.

### 1.2 A Tate \(3\)-torsion model

\[

\[
oxed{

## 10. Difficulty assessment versus \(N=15,21,18\)

### Arithmetic difficulty

Once the \(X_0(35)\) model is accepted, \(35\) is **closer to \(15/21\) than to \(18\)**:

- the decisive quotient is elliptic;
- the elliptic curve has rank \(0\) and only three rational points;
- the noncuspidal-looking quotient point fails to lift over \(\mathbf Q\) by the quadratic equation \(z^2+z-1=0\);
- no genus-\(2\), genus-\(3\), or genus-\(25\) Jacobian arithmetic is needed.

### Coordinate difficulty

It is nevertheless not a literal repeat of \(N=21\).  For \(21\), \(X_0(21)\) itself is genus \(1\).  For \(35\), the path from the raw Tate model includes

1. normalization of the \(\psi_5+\) square system;
2. a degree-\(12\) diamond quotient to \(X_0(35)\);
3. a further degree-\(2\) Atkin--Lehner quotient.

The second and third steps are mathematically routine, but the first coordinate transformation can have large numerators and denominators.

### Does the same “2-adic separatedness” template apply?

**At the finite-point/fiber layer, yes.**  Once the point lands on a rank-zero elliptic target, the same architecture used for \(N=21\) applies: enumerate the finite Mordell--Weil group, separate the finitely many target points, and exclude each fiber by explicit polynomial conditions.

**For proving rank \(0\), do not force a specifically \(2\)-adic proof.**  The target has rational \(3\)-torsion, and the natural small descent is a \(3\)-isogeny descent.  If the \(N=21\) framework treats the rank-zero point list as an already certified input, it can be reused unchanged.  If it formalizes the descent internally, \(35\mathrm a1\) should use the \(3\)-isogeny model above.

### Scheduling verdict

- **With abstract modular curves or an imported \(X_0(35)\) model:** days-scale after \(N=15/N=21\).
- **With a pre-generated Kubert bridge:** also days-scale for Lean verification; the bridge identity and all downstream identities are mechanical.
- **Requiring Codex alone to discover the Kubert bridge by hand:** not a good dispatch.  This is likely a multi-day CAS reconnaissance followed by days of formal verification, and could become a week-scale task depending on coefficient size.

So the correct verdict is:

> \(N=35\) has an elementary rank-zero elliptic quotient proof, but the direct Kubert-coordinate realization is not presently a hand-sized N21-style identity.  Generate that bridge once, certify it by ideal membership, and the rest is short.

## 11. Concrete acceptance checklist for the generated bridge

Before dispatching the Lean proof, require the CAS job to deliver all of the following:

1. Explicit integer polynomials \(Z_0,Z_1,W_0,W_1\).
2. A stated saturation factor \(S\), with every factor assigned to a cusp, discriminant, or coordinate-denominator locus.
3. Explicit \(R_1,R_2\) for the bridge identity.
4. Factorizations of the exceptional loci \(Z_0W_0D_hS=0\) modulo the two source equations.
5. Images of all source cusps, to prevent an omitted projective chart.
6. Verification that the proper AL involution becomes
   
   \[
   (z,w)\mapsto(-1/z,w/z^4)
   \]
   
   or its composition with the hyperelliptic involution.
7. The images of the three quotient points \(O,(1,3),(1,-4)\), with the fibers factored over \(\mathbf Q\).
8. A machine-readable file containing the polynomials, separate from the human derivation.

With those artifacts, the Lean side is almost entirely `field_simp`, `ring`, factor nonvanishing, and the previously certified rational-point list for \(35\mathrm a1\).
