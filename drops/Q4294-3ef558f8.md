# Combined \(2\)-and-\(3\) descent for excluding rational \(18\)-torsion

## Bottom line

The combined condition is genuinely stronger than either condition separately, but its exact effect is simply to cut the genus-zero \(X_1(9)\) parameter line by one rational-root condition and thereby produce **the genus-two curve \(X_1(18)\) itself**.

There is a useful explicit combined plane model:

\[
oxed{
F_{18}(t,p)
:=t(t-1)igl(t(1+p)^2-4p^2igr)+p(p-1)^2=0.
}
	ag{1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
oxed{

}
	ag{2}
\]

For \(t

e0\), a solution gives the rational \(x\)-coordinate of the order-two point explicitly:

\[
oxed{
 x_2=rac{t^2(t-1)(1+p)^2}{4p}.
}
	ag{3}
\]

The corresponding \(y\)-coordinate on the Tate model is

\[
oxed{
 y_2=rac{b-(1-c)x_2}{2}.
}
	ag{4}
\]

Thus (1) is a completely explicit formulation of “a rational point of order \(9\) and a rational point of order \(2\).” Since \(2\) and \(9\) are coprime, such points combine to a rational point of order \(18\), and conversely an order-\(18\) point gives both.

However, (1) does **not** yield a new elementary congruence or Fermat-type descent. Its smooth projective normalization is \(X_1(18)\), hence is birational to

\[
Y^2=U^6+4U^5+10U^4+10U^3+5U^2+2U+1.
	ag{5}
\]

The proposed mixed descents behave as follows.

1. \(\mathbf Z[\zeta_6]\) is exactly \(\mathbf Z[\zeta_3]\), so it supplies no new field beyond the already-considered Eisenstein descent. Moreover, \(6\)-Kummer data splits into its \(2\)- and \(3\)-primary data; there is no genuinely new mixed square/cube class.
2. A \(6\)-isogeny descent factors through the \(2\)- and \(3\)-isogenies. Its Selmer condition is a compatibility/fibre-product version of the two separate descents, not a new obstruction. Rational \(18\)-isogenies themselves occur, so isogeny existence is far too weak.
3. The explicit combined equation (1) is the strongest elementary reduction. Proving that its only rational points are the six cusps is exactly the global genus-two rational-point problem. The equation has noncuspidal points over every completion near a rational cusp, so no single local congruence can exclude it.

Therefore there is **no independent elementary combined \(2\)-\(3\) shortcut produced by (a), (b), or (c)**. The combined model (1) is valuable as a small algebraic bridge to \(X_1(18)\), but the final exclusion still needs a global certificate: Jacobian \(2\)-descent/rank zero, an equivalent elliptic-factor calculation over \(\mathbf Q(\zeta_3)\), or an equally strong global rational-point computation.

## Exact equivalence with order \(18\)

- If \(P_9\) has order \(9\) and \(Q_2\) has order \(2\), then \(P_9+Q_2\) has order \(18\).
- Conversely, from a point \(R\) of order \(18\), the points \(2R\) and \(9R\) have orders \(9\) and \(2\).

Thus the noncuspidal rational points of (27) are exactly the desired order-\(18\) structures, modulo the harmless choice of generator of the order-\(9\) subgroup.

The four obvious affine boundary points

\[
(t,p)=(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)
\]

are cuspidal; the other two cusps occur at infinity on the normalization. This matches the six rational cusps on the hyperelliptic model (5).

# 3. Why a \(\mathbf Z[\zeta_6]\) descent does not add a new mixed obstruction

## 3.1 The ring is not new

A primitive sixth root satisfies

\[
\zeta_6=1+\zeta_3,
\]

so

\[
oxed{
\mathbf Z[\zeta_6]=\mathbf Z[\zeta_3].
}
	ag{28}
\]

This is the Eisenstein integer ring. It has class number one and unit group \(\mu_6\). The prime \(3\) ramifies, while \(2\) is inert. Therefore adjoining \(\zeta_6\) diagonalizes the cubic/three-primary phenomena, but it does **not** split the two-division cubic.

Indeed, the discriminant squareclass of the two-division cubic is the elliptic discriminant squareclass. From (12), after removing squares, this varies with

\[
t(t-1)(t^2-t+1)(t^3-6t^2+3t+1).
	ag{29}
\]

It is not made a square merely by adjoining \(\sqrt{-3}\). Thus the rational two-torsion condition does not become a linear factor condition over \(\mathbf Q(\zeta_3)(t)\).

## 3.2 Sixth-power Kummer theory splits into square and cube theory

Over \(K=\mathbf Q(\zeta_6)\), the group schemes satisfy

\[
\mu_6\simeq\mu_2	imes\mu_3.
\]

Consequently

\[
oxed{
K^\*/K^{\*6}\simeq
(K^\*/K^{\*2})	imes(K^\*/K^{\*3}).
}
	ag{30}
\]

At the ideal level this is also just Chinese-remainder splitting of valuations modulo \(2\) and modulo \(3\). The only compatibility left is a finite unit in \(\mu_6\). Thus a “sixth-power descent” over the Eisenstein integers is not a new descent: it is the product of the squareclass and cubeclass descents with a tiny unit bookkeeping layer.

That compatibility can reduce a naively overlarge Cartesian list of candidates, but it cannot manufacture a new Fermat equation or remove the need to prove global completeness of the surviving candidates.

## 3.3 The combined plane equation does not factor into fixed coprime pieces

Equation (27) can be written

\[
t(t-1)H=-p(p-1)^2,
\qquad
H=t(1+p)^2-4p^2.
	ag{31}
\]

One might hope to use unique factorization and force the factors to be squares/cubes/sixth powers. The mixed gcds prevent this. Formally,

\[
H\equiv -4p^2\pmod t,
	ag{32}
\]

whereas

\[
H\equiv(1-p)(1+3p)\pmod{t-1}.
	ag{33}
\]

The factor \(1+3p\) carries arbitrary prime divisors. Hence the common-prime support is not confined to \(\{2,3\}\), even over the Eisenstein integers. There is no finite fixed squareclass/cubeclass list obtained merely from pairwise gcds. A complete descent from (31) recreates nontrivial covers of the same genus-two curve.

### Verdict for (a)

\[
oxed{
	ext{\(\mathbf Z[\zeta_6]\)-descent gives no new elementary obstruction.}
}
\]

It is either the already-failed Eisenstein \(3\)-descent plus a squareclass condition, or a full global descent on \(X_1(18)\)/its Jacobian in different notation.

# 4. The \(6\)-isogeny chain

Let \(P_9\) be rational of order \(9\), and let \(Q_2\) be rational of order \(2\). Put

\[
C_2=\langle Q_2angle,\qquad

\[

The composite is the degree-\(6\) isogeny with kernel \(C_6\).

## 4.1 Explicit degree-two stage

After completing the square, translating the rational two-torsion root to \(x=0\), and scaling, one obtains

\[
E_r:\quad y^2=x^3+Ax^2+Bx.
	ag{35}
\]

The standard quotient by \((0,0)\) is

\[

	ag{36}

\[
\phi_2(x,y)=\left(rac{y^2}{x^2},
rac{y(B-x^2)}{x^2}ight),
	ag{37}

Because \(\gcd(2,9)=1\), the point \(\phi_2(P_9)\) still has order \(9\). This is automatic and supplies **no additional equation** beyond (27).

The degree-three quotient in (34) can be written by Vélu's formulas using the rational subgroup generated by \(3\phi_2(P_9)\). Again, all its coefficients are rational functions of \(t,p\), but its existence is automatic once (27) holds.

## 4.2 Cohomological splitting

As rational Galois modules,

\[
C_6\simeq C_2	imes C_3.
\]

Therefore

\[
H^1(\mathbf Q,C_6)\simeq
H^1(\mathbf Q,C_2)	imes H^1(\mathbf Q,C_3),
	ag{38}
\]

and similarly locally. The composite \(6\)-isogeny Selmer condition is a fibre product of the \(2\)- and \(3\)-isogeny conditions, with the intermediate-curve compatibility supplied by (34).

This may be computationally preferable to two completely unrelated searches, but mathematically it is not a new obstruction. It is exactly “do both descents and retain compatible pairs.” Any proof that all compatible pairs vanish is already a genuine global Selmer computation.

There is also a basic moduli warning: rational cyclic \(18\)-isogenies exist over \(\mathbf Q\). Thus even a complete proof that \(E\) has a rational \(6\)- or \(18\)-isogeny says nothing close to the desired exclusion. What fails is the existence of a **rational generator**, not the existence of the rational cyclic subgroup.

To close it one must compute the corresponding global Selmer fibre product, which is comparable in substance to the existing global rank-zero certificate.

# 5. Is there a direct congruence obstruction on the combined equation?

## 5.1 No single local obstruction

At the rational cusp \((t,p)=(0,0)\),

\[
rac{\partial F_{18}}{\partial p}(0,0)=1.
	ag{39}
\]

Hence the cusp is smooth, and the implicit-function theorem over every completion \(\mathbf Q_v\) gives a one-dimensional \(v\)-adic neighbourhood of solutions. Taking \(t
e0\) sufficiently small gives noncuspidal local points.

Therefore

\[
oxed{
X_1(18)(\mathbf Q_v)	ext{ contains noncuspidal points for every place }v.
}
	ag{40}
\]

No congruence at a single prime, no real-sign argument, and no Hasse-principle obstruction on the original curve can prove the global exclusion.

## 5.2 The rational-root theorem does not produce finitely many roots

For a fixed rational \(t\), the two-division condition is the cubic (16). Its leading coefficient is \(4\), but its constant coefficient is \(b(t)^2\). After writing \(t=m/n\) and clearing denominators, the possible rational-root numerators and denominators depend on arbitrary divisors of \(m,n\), and of \(m^2-mn+n^2\). There is no fixed list analogous to “the root is \(\pm1\).” The parameter \(p\) in (27) is precisely a rational parametrization of this moving divisibility problem.

## 5.3 The combined equation is the global obstruction

Equation (27) is indeed more restrictive than either input separately:

- \(X_1(9)\) alone is rational and is parametrized by \(t\);
- rational two-torsion alone is a rational-root condition on a cubic;
- imposing both gives the genus-two normalization \(X_1(18)\).

But the statement

\[
F_{18}(t,p)=0,\quad t
e0,1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quadot
	ag{41}
\]

is simply another form of

\[
X_1(18)(\mathbf Q)=\{	ext{six cusps}\}.
\]

Any descent proving (41) must distinguish global rational points from the everywhere-locally-soluble noncuspidal points near the cusps. That is exactly where the Jacobian/Mordell-Weil certificate enters.

# 6. What the combined calculation is still good for

Although it does not replace the rank-zero proof, (27) is useful for formalization.

1. **Small front-end model.** It avoids introducing genus-two coordinates until the final birational normalization step.
2. **Pure polynomial verification.** Equations (8), (12), (18)-(20), and (25)-(27) are all `ring`/`field_simp` identities.
3. **Exact moduli bridge.** A rational solution gives the order-two point by (3)-(4), hence an order-\(18\) point; conversely an order-\(18\) point produces a solution.
4. **Alternative input to a global certificate.** One may map (27) to the hyperelliptic model (5), or map it to a quotient curve and perform finite fibre elimination after a rank-zero theorem.

A Lean-facing theorem package could therefore have the shape

