# Q3947: rational points on `X_1(11)` / Cremona `11a3`

## Bottom line

For

\[
E : y^2 + y = x^3 - x^2
\]

over `ℚ`, the rational points are exactly

\[
E(\mathbb Q)=\{O,(0,0),(0,-1),(1,0),(1,-1)\}.
\]

Small correction to the wording in the prompt: if `E(ℚ)_tors ≃ ℤ/5ℤ`, then there are **five torsion points total**, including `O`; it is not “five affine torsion points plus infinity.”

The proof route I would use in Lean is:

1. Prove the displayed five points form a rational subgroup of order `5`.
2. Prove a rank-zero certificate, preferably as a separate theorem: the `2`-Selmer group of `E` is the singleton group.
3. Use good reduction at `2`: `#E(𝔽₂)=5`, so rational torsion injects into a group of order `5`.
4. Conclude `E(ℚ)=E(ℚ)_tors=< (0,0) >`, hence exactly the five listed points.

This is the shortest robust mathematical proof.  A simple denominator descent in the style of some obstruction-curve arguments is **not** the clean route here; it would essentially have to reprove the rank-zero descent.

## The model and the known five points

Use the generalized Weierstrass coefficients

\[
(a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4,a_6)=(0,-1,1,0,0),
\]

so

\[
y^2+a_1xy+a_3y=x^3+a_2x^2+a_4x+a_6
\]

is exactly

\[
y^2+y=x^3-x^2.
\]

The inverse formula on this model is

\[
-(x,y)=(x,-y-1).
\]

Let

\[
P=(0,0).
\]

Then

\[
-P=(0,-1).
\]

The tangent at `P` is `y=0`.  Intersecting `y=0` with the cubic gives

\[
0=x^3-x^2=x^2(x-1),
\]

so the third intersection point is `(1,0)`.  Therefore

\[
2P=-(1,0)=(1,-1).
\]

The line through `P=(0,0)` and `2P=(1,-1)` is `y=-x`.  Substitution gives

\[
x^2-x=x^3-x^2,
\]

hence

\[
x(x-1)^2=0.
\]

The third intersection is again `(1,-1)`, so

\[
3P=-(1,-1)=(1,0).
\]

Then

\[
4P=-P=(0,-1), \qquad 5P=O.
\]

Thus the five listed points are precisely

\[
O,\ P,\ 2P,\ 3P,\ 4P.
\]

## Discriminant and good reduction at `2`

For this model,

\[
\begin{aligned}
b_2 &= a_1^2+4a_2=-4,\\
b_4 &= 2a_4+a_1a_3=0,\\
b_6 &= a_3^2+4a_6=1,\\
b_8 &= a_1^2a_6+4a_2a_6-a_1a_3a_4+a_2a_3^2-a_4^2=-1.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence

\[
\Delta=-b_2^2b_8-8b_4^3-27b_6^2+9b_2b_4b_6=16-27=-11.
\]

So the curve has good reduction at every prime `p ≠ 11`, in particular at `p=2`.

Over `𝔽₂`, the equation is

\[
y^2+y=x^3-x^2=x^3+x^2.
\]

For `x=0` and `x=1`, the right hand side is `0`; for each of these two `x` values, both `y=0` and `y=1` solve `y^2+y=0`.  Thus there are four affine points and the point at infinity:

\[
#E(\mathbb F_2)=5.
\]

Since reduction at a prime of good reduction injects the prime-to-`p` torsion subgroup into the reduced curve, and since `#E(𝔽₂)=5` is odd, all rational torsion injects into `E(𝔽₂)`.  Therefore

\[
#E(\mathbb Q)_{\mathrm{tors}} \mid 5.
\]

But the point `P=(0,0)` has order `5`, so

\[
E(\mathbb Q)_{\mathrm{tors}} \cong \mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z.
\]

Once rank zero is known, this already gives the desired list.

## The rank-zero step

Completing the square is fine:

\[
z=2y+1
\]

gives

\[
z^2=4x^3-4x^2+1.
\]

However, the sentence

> the `2`-Selmer group is trivial because the `2`-torsion has discriminant `-44`, not a square

is not valid.  The discriminant calculation only tells you information about the rational `2`-torsion field.  In particular, after setting `β=2x`, the `2`-torsion polynomial is

\[
β^3-2β^2+2,
\]

whose discriminant is `-44`.  The fact that this cubic has no rational root proves

\[
E(\mathbb Q)[2]=0,
\]

but it does **not** by itself compute the `2`-Selmer group.

The correct `2`-descent statement is this:

> A full `2`-descent on `z^2=4x^3-4x^2+1`, equivalently over the cubic field `K=ℚ(β)` with `β^3-2β^2+2=0`, gives
>
> \[
> \operatorname{Sel}^{(2)}(E/\mathbb Q)=0
> \]
>
> as an `𝔽₂`-vector space, i.e. the Selmer set/group has only the identity class.

Then the usual exact sequence gives

\[
E(\mathbb Q)/2E(\mathbb Q)=0,
\]

because `E(ℚ)[2]=0`.  By the Mordell-Weil theorem, `E(ℚ)` is finitely generated; for a finitely generated abelian group,

\[
E(\mathbb Q)/2E(\mathbb Q) \cong (\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^r \oplus E(\mathbb Q)[2].
\]

Since this quotient is trivial and `E(ℚ)[2]=0`, the rank is

\[
r=0.
\]

Combining rank zero with the torsion computation above gives

\[
E(\mathbb Q)=E(\mathbb Q)_{\mathrm{tors}}=<P>,
\]

so the rational points are exactly

\[
O,(0,0),(1,-1),(1,0),(0,-1).
\]

This is the clean proof.

## What the `2`-descent certificate should contain

For a self-contained formal proof, the `2`-descent should not be a black-box appeal to Cremona tables.  The certificate can be made finite and checkable.

Use

\[
C: z^2=4x^3-4x^2+1.
\]

Let

\[
K=\mathbb Q(β), \qquad β^3-2β^2+2=0.
\]

Then the usual `2`-descent map for an irreducible cubic sends a rational point to the squareclass of `2x-β` in `K^*/K^{*2}` with square norm.  The finite computation is supported only at primes above `2` and `11`.

A Lean-friendly descent certificate would consist of these finite facts:

1. `𝓞_K = ℤ[β]`.
2. The class group of `K` is trivial.
3. The unit group is generated modulo squares by `-1` and one fundamental unit, for example the class of `β-1`.
4. Factor the primes above `2` and `11` in `𝓞_K`.
5. Enumerate the resulting finite group of `S`-squareclasses with square rational norm.
6. For every non-identity candidate squareclass, prove that the associated `2`-covering has no point over at least one of `ℚ₂`, `ℚ₁₁`, or `ℝ`.
7. Conclude that the only everywhere locally soluble class is the identity.

This is a finite certificate.  It is absolutely formalizable in Lean in principle, but it is a real algebraic-number-theory development: rings of integers for one cubic field, class group/unit computations, prime ideal factorization, and local solubility checks.  It is much larger than the final point-enumeration argument.

## Why denominator descent is not the simple path

Write a rational point in normalized denominator form

\[
x=\frac{a}{b^2}, \qquad y=\frac{c}{b^3}, \qquad \gcd(a,b)=\gcd(c,b)=1.
\]

Substitution gives

\[
c^2+c b^3=a^3-a^2b^2=a^2(a-b^2).
\]

Equivalently, after completing the square,

\[
(2c+b^3)^2=4a^3-4a^2b^2+b^6.
\]

Unlike some obstruction-curve denominator descents, this equation does not immediately force `b=1`.  For an odd prime `p | b`, reducing modulo `p` gives only

\[
c^2 \equiv a^3 \pmod p,
\]

so it merely imposes a quadratic-residue condition on `a`; it is not a contradiction.  The missing argument is precisely a global descent/rank-zero argument.

So the answer to “can it be done by denominator descent like the `ObstructionN14` pattern?” is:

*Not as a short local denominator argument.*  One could package a full descent in denominator language, but then it is essentially the same work as a `2`-descent or an isogeny descent.  For the Lean project, I would not try to prove this theorem by ad hoc denominator manipulation unless such a descent certificate already exists in the codebase.

## Independent computational check

Here is a Sage script that verifies the numerical facts.  This is not a replacement for the Lean proof, but it is a useful sanity check for the intended certificate.

```python
from sage.all import EllipticCurve, GF, QQ

# y^2 + y = x^3 - x^2
# Sage coefficients are [a1, a2, a3, a4, a6].
E = EllipticCurve(QQ, [0, -1, 1, 0, 0])

assert E.discriminant() == -11
assert E.rank() == 0
assert E.torsion_order() == 5

pts = set(E.gens() + E.torsion_points())
expected = {
    E(0),
    E([0, 0]),
    E([0, -1]),
    E([1, 0]),
    E([1, -1]),
}
assert pts == expected

E2 = E.change_ring(GF(2))
assert E2.cardinality() == 5

print("E(Q) = {O, (0,0), (0,-1), (1,0), (1,-1)}")
```

## Lean formalization recommendation

I would split the Lean development into two layers.

First, isolate the hard arithmetic as one named theorem/certificate:

```lean
import Mathlib.Tactic

/-
Shape only: the actual project names for rational points, Selmer groups,
and Weierstrass curves will differ.
-/

axiom E11a3_twoSelmer_rank_zero_certificate :
  True
```

In the real project this should not remain an axiom; replace it by either:

* a proved `2`-descent certificate for the cubic field `β^3 - 2β^2 + 2`, or
* a trusted project theorem saying that Cremona `11a3` has Mordell-Weil rank `0`.

Second, make the final enumeration theorem elementary:

1. Define `E11a3 : WeierstrassCurve ℚ` with coefficients `(0,-1,1,0,0)`.
2. Define the five points `O`, `(0,0)`, `(0,-1)`, `(1,0)`, `(1,-1)`.
3. Prove by direct `norm_num`/ring normalization that the four affine points satisfy the equation.
4. Prove the tangent-chord computations giving the order-`5` cycle.
5. Compute `Δ=-11` and `#E(𝔽₂)=5`.
6. Use the standard good-reduction torsion-injection theorem to show `#E(ℚ)_tors ∣ 5`.
7. Use the rank-zero certificate to replace `E(ℚ)` by its torsion subgroup.
8. Conclude by finite enumeration of the cyclic subgroup generated by `(0,0)`.

That final layer is very feasible in Lean.  The only genuinely heavy part is the descent certificate.  The proposed completing-square step is fine as the entry point to that certificate, but the proof should explicitly compute the full `2`-Selmer group; it should not infer it from the nonsquare discriminant of the `2`-torsion polynomial.
