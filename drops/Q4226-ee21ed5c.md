# \(X_1(21)\): explicit quotient to \(X_0(21)\), elementary descent, and the stronger \(G_{21}=0\) exclusion

## Executive verdict

There are two important corrections to the proposed route.

1. The map \(X_1(21)	o X_0(21)\) has degree
   
   \[
   \varphi(21)/2=6,
   \]
   
   not degree \(2\).  On the affine chart in the question it factors as
   
   \[
   X_1(21)\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow X_0(21),
   \qquad \deg=2\cdot 3,
   \]
   
   where \(C\) is the normalization of \(G_{21}(t,X)=0\).  The first map forgets the choice of generator of the order-\(3\) subgroup; the second map forgets the choice of generator of the order-\(7\) subgroup.  The degree-\(3\) map is the quotient by the order-\(3\) diamond action \(P_7\mapsto 2P_7\).  It is not an Atkin--Lehner quotient.

2. The optimal curve 21a1 is
   
   \[
   E_0:\quad y^2+xy=x^3-4x-1,
   \]
   
   but
   
   \[
   E_0(\mathbb Q)\cong \mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z,
   \]
   
   not \(\mathbb Z/8\mathbb Z\).  The curve has three nonzero rational \(2\)-torsion points.  The official Cremona data confirms the model, rank \(0\), and torsion order \(8\); see `JohnCremona/ecdata`, `docs/curves.1-1000.html`, conductor \(21\), row A1.  Below I give an explicit descent which does not use the database's rank assertion.

The useful conclusion is stronger than the original target:

> **Stronger theorem.** If \(t,X\in\mathbb Q\), \(t\ne0\), and \(t\ne1\), then
> \[
> G_{21}(t,X)\ne0.
> \]

Hence the condition \(Z^2=D_{21}(t,X)\) is unnecessary.  In particular the desired \(X_1(21)(\mathbb Q)\) noncuspidal exclusion follows immediately.

The proof is completely explicit:

* construct a rational map \(C:G_{21}=0\to E_0\);
* prove \(E_0(\mathbb Q)\) is the eight-point group by a \(2\)-isogeny descent plus a \(2\)-adic separatedness argument;
* evaluate the finitely many fibers and use the rational-root theorem.

## 1. The Tate family, \(D_{21}\), and \(G_{21}\)

Put

\[
 a_1=1+t-t^2,\qquad a_2=a_3=t^2(1-t),\qquad a_4=a_6=0.
\]

The underlying Tate-normal-form family is

\[
 E_t:\quad y^2+(1+t-t^2)xy+t^2(1-t)y=x^3+t^2(1-t)x^2.
\]

Equivalently, in the usual Tate notation

\[
 b=t^2(t-1),\qquad c=t(t-1),
\]

this is

\[
 y^2+(1-c)xy-by=x^3-bx^2,
\]

and \(P=(0,0)\) has order \(7\) on the nonsingular fibers.

The completed-square coordinate

\[
 Z=2y+a_1X+a_3
\]

satisfies

\[
 Z^2=D_{21}(t,X),
\]

where

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{21}(t,X)={}&4X^3+(t^4-6t^3+3t^2+2t+1)X^2\\
&+(2t^5-4t^4+2t^2)X+t^6-2t^5+t^4.
\end{aligned}
\]

Indeed, with the standard invariants

\[
\begin{aligned}
b_2&=t^4-6t^3+3t^2+2t+1,\\
b_4&=t^5-2t^4+t^2,\\
b_6&=t^6-2t^5+t^4,
\end{aligned}
\]

one has \(D_{21}=4X^3+b_2X^2+2b_4X+b_6\).

The \(3\)-division polynomial is

\[
\psi_3(X)=3X^4+b_2X^3+3b_4X^2+3b_6X+b_8,
\]

with

\[
 b_8=t^6(1-t)^3=-t^9+3t^8-3t^7+t^6.
\]

Thus \(\psi_3=G_{21}\) exactly.

The discriminant of the Tate family is

\[
 \Delta_t=t^7(t-1)^7(t^3-8t^2+5t+1).
\]

For rational \(t\ne0,1\), the remaining cubic factor cannot vanish: it is monic with constant term \(1\), and its only possible integral roots are \(\pm1\), neither of which is a root.  Therefore all rational fibers considered below are nonsingular.

The equation \(G_{21}=0\) says that the \(3\)-division polynomial has a rational \(x\)-coordinate, equivalently that \(E_t\) has a rational cyclic subgroup of order \(3\).  The extra square condition \(Z^2=D_{21}\) chooses an actual rational generator of that subgroup.

## 2. Correct modular diagram and the order-\(3\) diamond action

Let \(C\) be the normalization of the affine curve \(G_{21}(t,X)=0\).  Moduli-theoretically \(C\) records

\[
 (E,P_7,C_3),
\]

where \(P_7\) is a point of order \(7\) and \(C_3\) is a cyclic subgroup of order \(3\).

The factorization is

\[
 X_1(21)\xrightarrow{\ 2\ }C\xrightarrow{\ 3\ }X_0(21).
\]

For the first map, if \(R\) generates the order-\(21\) subgroup, send

\[
 R\longmapsto (3R,\langle7R\rangle).
\]

For a fixed pair \((P_7,C_3)\), the two choices of generator of \(C_3\) give the two points in the generic fiber.

For the second map, changing the chosen order-\(7\) generator by \(P_7\mapsto2P_7\) gives the order-\(3\) transformation

\[
 \sigma(t,X)=
 \left(
   \frac{t-1}{t},
   \frac{X-t^2(t-1)}{t^4}
 \right).
\]

One checks directly that \(\sigma^3=1\) and

\[
 t^{16}G_{21}(\sigma(t,X))=G_{21}(t,X).
\]

The \(t\)-coordinate action is the familiar three-cycle

\[
 t\longmapsto\frac{t-1}{t}\longmapsto\frac1{1-t}\longmapsto t.
\]

This is the actual diamond quotient needed here.

## 3. Explicit invariant functions and the quotient equation

Define

\[
 q=t^2-t+1,
\]

and, for \(t\ne0,1\),

\[
 s=\frac{t^3-3t+1}{t(t-1)},
\]

\[
 r=\frac{(t^2-t+1)X-t^2(t-1)}{t^2(t-1)^2}.
\]

Both \(s\) and \(r\) are invariant under \(\sigma\).  The first is the trace of the order-\(7\) Tate parameter; the second is an affine trace of the \(3\)-subgroup coordinate.

Put

\[
 R_{21}(s,r)=
 3r^4+(s^2-7s+9)r^3+6(s-3)r^2+12r-s.
\]

The key exact certificate is

\[
\boxed{
 t^8(t-1)^8R_{21}(s(t),r(t,X))
 =(t^2-t+1)^4G_{21}(t,X).
}
\]

This is a literal polynomial identity after clearing denominators.  It is ideal for Lean: `field_simp` followed by `ring` proves it.

Consequently

\[
 G_{21}(t,X)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad R_{21}(s,r)=0.
\]

The parameter \(t\) is recovered over the quotient by the cubic

\[
\boxed{
 t^3-st^2+(s-3)t+1=0.
}
\]

Indeed this is another immediate cleared-denominator identity from the definition of \(s\).

### Derivation check

Writing \(p=t(t-1)\) and \(v=X/p\), the equation \(G_{21}=0\) becomes, after division by \(p^3t^3\),

\[
 3tpu^4+\bigl((p-1)^2-4tp\bigr)u^3+3(p-1)u^2+3u-1=0
\]

with \(u=X/(t^2(t-1))\).  Reducing by

\[
 t^3-st^2+(s-3)t+1=0
\]

produces exactly the displayed \(R_{21}\).  No modular-symbol or black-box model identification is being used.

## 4. Explicit map from the quotient to 21a1

Set

\[
 d=r^2-r+1.
\]

Over \(\mathbb Q\), \(d\ne0\), because

\[
 4d=(2r-1)^2+3>0.
\]

Define

\[
 w=\frac{2r^3s-7r^3+6r^2-1}{r^2-r+1}.
\]

The discriminant of \(R_{21}\), regarded as a quadratic in \(s\), factors as

\[
\begin{aligned}
&(-7r^3+6r^2-1)^2
 -4r^3(3r^4+9r^3-18r^2+12r)\\
&\qquad=-(r+1)(3r-1)(4r+1)(r^2-r+1)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Equivalently, the exact identity is

\[
\boxed{
 (r^2-r+1)^2
 \left(w^2+(r+1)(3r-1)(4r+1)\right)
 =4r^3R_{21}(s,r).
}
\]

Thus, on \(R_{21}=0\),

\[
 w^2=-(r+1)(3r-1)(4r+1).
\]

Now put

\[
 x=-3r-1,
\qquad
 y=\frac{3w+3r+1}{2}.
\]

Then

\[
 2y+x=3w
\]

and

\[
 (x+2)(x-2)(4x+1)
 =-9(r+1)(3r-1)(4r+1).
\]

Therefore

\[
 (2y+x)^2=(x+2)(x-2)(4x+1),
\]

which is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{
 y^2+xy=x^3-4x-1.
}
\]

Hence the requested explicit map is the following nested rational map:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
s&=\frac{t^3-3t+1}{t(t-1)},\\[2mm]
r&=\frac{(t^2-t+1)X-t^2(t-1)}{t^2(t-1)^2},\\[2mm]
w&=\frac{2r^3s-7r^3+6r^2-1}{r^2-r+1},\\[2mm]
x&=-3r-1,\\[1mm]
y&=\frac{3w+3r+1}{2}.
\end{aligned}
}
\]

The \(x\)-coordinate can also be written directly as

\[
 x=
 \frac{t^2(t-1)(4-t)-3(t^2-t+1)X}
      {t^2(t-1)^2}.
\]

This is the explicit \(C\to X_0(21)\cong21\mathrm{a}1\) map asked for in part (1), with the degree and quotient corrected.

## 5. Explicit determination of \(E_0(\mathbb Q)\)

Let

\[
 E_0:\quad y^2+xy=x^3-4x-1.
\]

Completing the square gives

\[
 (2y+x)^2=(x+2)(x-2)(4x+1).
\]

The eight rational points are

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
E_0(\mathbb Q)=\{&O,
(-2,1),(2,-1),(-\tfrac14,\tfrac18),\\
&(-1,-1),(-1,2),(5,8),(5,-13)\}.
\end{aligned}
}
\]

The first three affine points are the nonzero \(2\)-torsion points.  If

\[
 P=(-1,-1),\qquad T=(-2,1),
\]

then

\[
 2P=(2,-1),\qquad P+T=(5,8),
\]

and

\[
 T+(2,-1)=(-\tfrac14,\tfrac18).
\]

Thus these eight points form a subgroup

\[
 H=\langle P,T\rangle\cong\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z.
\]

The remaining task is to prove there are no other points without assuming rank \(0\).

### 5.1 A convenient \(2\)-isogeny pair

Set

\[
 u=4(x-2),\qquad W=4(2y+x).
\]

Then \(E_0\) is isomorphic over \(\mathbb Q\) to

\[
 E_1:\quad W^2=u(u+9)(u+16)=u^3+25u^2+144u.
\]

Quotienting by the rational point \((0,0)\) gives the \(2\)-isogenous curve

\[
 E_2:\quad V^2=v^3-50v^2+49v=v(v-1)(v-49).
\]

For \(u\ne0\), the isogeny is

\[
 \phi(u,W)=
 \left(
   \frac{W^2}{u^2},
   \frac{W(144-u^2)}{u^2}
 \right).
\]

For \(v\ne0\), the dual isogeny is

\[
 \widehat\phi(v,V)=
 \left(
   \frac{V^2}{4v^2},
   \frac{V(49-v^2)}{8v^2}
 \right).
\]

A direct calculation gives

\[
 \widehat\phi\circ\phi=[2]_{E_1},
 \qquad
 \phi\circ\widehat\phi=[2]_{E_2}.
\]

### 5.2 Kummer images

For a curve

\[
 Y^2=X^3+aX^2+bX
\]

with rational \((0,0)\), define the Kummer square class by

\[
 \alpha(O)=1,\qquad \alpha(0,0)=b,
 \qquad \alpha(X,Y)=X\pmod{\mathbb Q^{\times2}}.
\]

A squarefree representative \(d\mid b\) occurs exactly when the quartic

\[
 N^2=dM^4+aM^2e^2+\frac bd e^4
\]

has a primitive nonzero integer solution.  This follows by writing

\[
 X=d(M/e)^2
\]

in lowest terms.

For \(E_1\), \(a=25\), \(b=144\), so the possible squarefree classes are

\[
 d\in\{\pm1,\pm2,\pm3,\pm6\}.
\]

The four classes

\[
 1,-1,-3,3
\]

occur, with witnesses

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
d&(M,e)&N\\ \hline
1&(0,1)&12\\
-1&(4,1)&0\\
-3&(2,1)&2\\
3&(2,1)&14
\end{array}
\]

and are represented on \(E_1\) by

\[
 O,\quad(-16,0),\quad(-12,-12),\quad(12,84).
\]

The even classes \(d=\pm2,\pm6\) are impossible by a uniform mod-\(8/16\) argument.

The equation is

\[
 N^2=dM^4+25M^2e^2+(144/d)e^4.
\]

For primitive \(M,e\):

* If \(M\) is odd and \(e\) is even, the right side is \(2\) or \(6\pmod8\).
* If \(M,e\) are both odd, it is \(3\) or \(7\pmod8\).
* If \(M\) is even, then \(e\) is odd.  Modulo \(16\), \(M^4\equiv0\), \(25M^2\equiv0\) or \(4\), and \((144/d)e^4\equiv8\).  The right side is therefore \(8\) or \(12\pmod{16}\).

None is a square.  Consequently

\[
\boxed{
 \operatorname{im}(\alpha_1)=\{1,-1,-3,3\}.
}
\]

For \(E_2\), \(a=-50\), \(b=49\), so the possible classes are

\[
 d\in\{\pm1,\pm7\}.
\]

The classes \(-1\) and \(-7\) give negative-definite quartics and are impossible.  For \(d=7\),

\[
 N^2=7M^4-50M^2e^2+7e^4.
\]

If exactly one of \(M,e\) is even, the right side is \(7\pmod8\).  If both are odd, then modulo \(16\)

\[
 M^4=e^4=1,\qquad M^2e^2\in\{1,9\},
\]

and the right side is always

\[
 7+7+14\equiv12\pmod{16}.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
 \operatorname{im}(\alpha_2)=\{1\}.
}
\]

### 5.3 The exact coset statement

The standard kernel statements can be proved directly from the displayed isogeny formulas:

\[
 \alpha_1(Q)=1\iff Q\in\widehat\phi(E_2(\mathbb Q)),
\]

\[
 \alpha_2(Q')=1\iff Q'\in\phi(E_1(\mathbb Q)).
\]

For example, if \(u=z^2\) on \(E_1\), then a preimage under \(\widehat\phi\) is obtained by solving

\[
 v^2-(4u+50)v+49=0.
\]

Its discriminant is

\[
 16(u^2+25u+144)=\left(\frac{4W}{z}\right)^2.
\]

The other kernel identity is analogous: if \(v=z^2\), solve

\[
 u^2+(25-v)u+144=0,
\]

whose discriminant is

\[
 v^2-50v+49=\left(\frac Vz\right)^2.
\]

Now take any \(Q\in E_1(\mathbb Q)\).  Choose one of the four explicit torsion representatives \(T_d\) with

\[
 \alpha_1(Q)=\alpha_1(T_d).
\]

Then \(\alpha_1(Q-T_d)=1\), so

\[
 Q-T_d=\widehat\phi(Q')
\]

for some \(Q'\in E_2(\mathbb Q)\).  Since \(\alpha_2(Q')=1\), write

\[
 Q'=\phi(R).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
 Q=T_d+2R.
}
\]

Thus every rational point is congruent modulo \(2E_1(\mathbb Q)\) to one of four points of the explicit eight-point torsion subgroup.

### 5.4 Axiom-free termination: \(2\)-adic separatedness

The preceding coset statement by itself must not be combined with an abstract “rank \(0\)” axiom.  Instead iterate it.

For every \(Q\in E_0(\mathbb Q)\) and every \(n\ge1\), there are

\[
 T_n\in H,\qquad Q_n\in E_0(\mathbb Q)
\]

such that

\[
 Q=T_n+2^nQ_n.
\]

The subgroup \(H\) has exponent \(4\), so

\[
 4Q=2^n(4Q_n)
\]

for every \(n\).

The discriminant of \(E_0\) is

\[
 \Delta(E_0)=3969=3^4\cdot7^2,
\]

so the model has good reduction at \(2\).  A direct count gives

\[
 \#E_0(\mathbb F_2)=4.
\]

Therefore \(4Q\) and every \(4Q_n\) lie in the formal kernel of reduction at \(2\).

Let \(z=-x/y\) be the standard local parameter at infinity.  On the formal kernel \(z\in2\mathbb Z_2\).  The formal doubling series has the form

\[
 [2](z)=2z+\sum_{k\ge2}c_kz^k,
 \qquad c_k\in\mathbb Z_2.
\]

Hence

\[
 v_2([2](z))\ge v_2(z)+1.
\]

If a formal-kernel point is divisible by \(2^n\) inside the formal kernel for every \(n\), its local parameter has arbitrarily large \(2\)-adic valuation, so it is the identity.  Applying this to \(4Q\) gives

\[
 4Q=O.
\]

Thus every rational point is \(4\)-torsion.  Finally, at the good prime \(5\), a direct count gives

\[
 \#E_0(\mathbb F_5)=8.
\]

Reduction at \(5\) is injective on \(2\)-power torsion.  Therefore

\[
 \#E_0(\mathbb Q)\le8.
\]

The eight displayed points already exist, so they exhaust \(E_0(\mathbb Q)\).

This proves the point list and the group structure without `AddGroup.FG`, Mordell--Weil, BSD, or a rank axiom.  Rank \(0\) is then a consequence.

## 6. Cusps and noncuspidal rational points of \(X_0(21)\)

The quotient parameter \(s\) maps \(X_0(21)\) to \(X_0(7)\).  The two cusps of \(X_0(7)\) occur at

\[
 s=\infty\quad\text{and}\quad s=8.
\]

The latter value follows immediately from

\[
 t^3-8t^2+5t+1=0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \frac{t^3-3t+1}{t(t-1)}=8.
\]

The four rational cusps on \(E_0\) are

\[
\boxed{
 O,\quad(-1,2),\quad(-2,1),\quad(5,-13).
}
\]

The four noncuspidal rational points are

\[
\boxed{
 (2,-1),\quad(-\tfrac14,\tfrac18),\quad(-1,-1),\quad(5,8).
}
\]

For later use, write

\[
 r=-\frac{x+1}{3},
 \qquad
 w=\frac{2y+x}{3}.
\]

The corresponding values are:

| \(E_0(\mathbb Q)\) point | cusp? | \(r\) | \(w\) | \(s\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(O\) | yes | \(\infty\) | \(\infty\) | \(\infty\) |
| \((-1,2)\) | yes | \(0\) | \(1\) | \(\infty\) |
| \((-2,1)\) | yes | \(1/3\) | \(0\) | \(8\) |
| \((5,-13)\) | yes | \(-2\) | \(-7\) | \(8\) |
| \((2,-1)\) | no | \(-1\) | \(0\) | \(6\) |
| \((-1/4,1/8)\) | no | \(-1/4\) | \(0\) | \(-33/2\) |
| \((-1,-1)\) | no | \(0\) | \(-1\) | \(0\) |

At \(r=0\), the affine formula defining \(w\) gives \(w=-1\), so the point \((-1,2)\), for which \(w=1\), is not in the image of this affine chart.  The point \(O\) is also not in the image because \(x=-3r-1\) is finite.

## 7. Explicit fibers and the direct proof that \(G_{21}=0\) has no rational point

For an affine rational point of \(C\), the value \(t\) must satisfy

\[
 f_s(T)=T^3-sT^2+(s-3)T+1=0.
\]

### Noncuspidal fibers

The four noncuspidal points give:

1. At \((2,-1)\), \(s=6\):
   
   \[
   T^3-6T^2+3T+1=0.
   \]
   
   It is monic with constant term \(1\), so a rational root must be \(\pm1\); the values are \(-1\) and \(-9\).

2. At \((-1/4,1/8)\), \(s=-33/2\):
   
   \[
   2T^3+33T^2-39T+2=0.
   \]
   
   The rational-root candidates are
   
   \[
   \pm1,\ \pm2,\ \pm\tfrac12,
   \]
   
   and the corresponding values are nonzero.  Equivalently, reduction modulo \(5\) has no root.

3. At \((-1,-1)\), \(s=0\):
   
   \[
   T^3-3T+1=0.
   \]
   
   The only possible rational roots are \(\pm1\), and neither works.  Reduction modulo \(2\) is \(T^3+T+1\), which has no root.

4. At \((5,8)\), \(s=15/8\):
   
   \[
   8T^3-15T^2-9T+8=0.
   \]
   
   The rational-root candidates are
   
   \[
   \pm1,\pm2,\pm4,\pm8,
   \pm\tfrac12,\pm\tfrac14,\pm\tfrac18,
   \]
   
   and none is a root.  Equivalently, reduction modulo \(5\) is
   
   \[
   3T^3+T+3,
   \]
   
   which has no root in \(\mathbb F_5\).

### Cuspidal target fibers

The two finite cusps \((-2,1)\) and \((5,-13)\) both have \(s=8\), so their possible \(t\)-values satisfy

\[
 T^3-8T^2+5T+1=0.
\]

Again the only possible rational roots are \(\pm1\), and neither works.

The other two target cusps are not in the finite affine image, as noted above.

Therefore every possible image point is excluded, proving

\[
\boxed{
 \forall t,X\in\mathbb Q,
 \quad t\ne0\to t\ne1\to G_{21}(t,X)\ne0.
}
\]

This is the requested “cheaper claim,” and it is true.

The argument is stronger than a small-height search: it excludes every height.  I did not rely on an external brute-force search; the finite fiber computation above is a complete exact search after the quotient.  Any bounded-height search must therefore return the empty set.

As an external sanity check only, the known classification of rational isogeny--torsion graphs also says that a rational \(7\)-torsion point cannot coexist with a rational \(3\)-isogeny.  That classification is much deeper and should not be used in the Lean proof.

## 8. Consequence for the original \(X_1(21)\) target

Suppose

\[
 t\ne0,\qquad t\ne1,\qquad G_{21}(t,X)=0,
 \qquad Z^2=D_{21}(t,X).
\]

The stronger theorem already contradicts \(G_{21}(t,X)=0\).  Hence

\[
\boxed{
 \nexists(t,X,Z)\in\mathbb Q^3,
 \quad t\ne0,1,
 \quad G_{21}(t,X)=0,
 \quad Z^2=D_{21}(t,X).
}
\]

No square analysis and no separate fiber lifting are needed.

## 9. Lean 4 proof architecture

The algebraic part is unusually friendly to Lean because the difficult maps come with small exact polynomial identities.

### 9.1 Definitions

```lean
import Mathlib

open Polynomial

namespace X1_21

noncomputable section

abbrev ℚ := Rat

def G21 (t X : ℚ) : ℚ :=
  3 * X^4
    + (t^4 - 6*t^3 + 3*t^2 + 2*t + 1) * X^3
    + (3*t^5 - 6*t^4 + 3*t^2) * X^2
    + (3*t^6 - 6*t^5 + 3*t^4) * X
    - t^9 + 3*t^8 - 3*t^7 + t^6

def D21 (t X : ℚ) : ℚ :=
  4 * X^3
    + (t^4 - 6*t^3 + 3*t^2 + 2*t + 1) * X^2
    + (2*t^5 - 4*t^4 + 2*t^2) * X
    + t^6 - 2*t^5 + t^4

def q7 (t : ℚ) : ℚ := t^2 - t + 1

def s21 (t : ℚ) : ℚ :=
  (t^3 - 3*t + 1) / (t * (t - 1))

def r21 (t X : ℚ) : ℚ :=
  ((t^2 - t + 1) * X - t^2 * (t - 1)) /
    (t^2 * (t - 1)^2)

def R21 (s r : ℚ) : ℚ :=
  3*r^4 + (s^2 - 7*s + 9)*r^3
    + 6*(s - 3)*r^2 + 12*r - s

def w21 (s r : ℚ) : ℚ :=
  (2*r^3*s - 7*r^3 + 6*r^2 - 1) / (r^2 - r + 1)

def e21x (r : ℚ) : ℚ := -3*r - 1

def e21y (s r : ℚ) : ℚ :=
  (3*w21 s r + 3*r + 1) / 2

def OnE21 (x y : ℚ) : Prop :=
  y^2 + x*y = x^3 - 4*x - 1
```

### 9.2 Cleared-denominator certificates

```lean
lemma s21_cubic (t : ℚ) (ht0 : t ≠ 0) (ht1 : t ≠ 1) :
    t^3 - s21 t * t^2 + (s21 t - 3) * t + 1 = 0 := by
  field_simp [s21, ht0, ht1]
  ring

lemma quotient_certificate (t X : ℚ)
    (ht0 : t ≠ 0) (ht1 : t ≠ 1) :
    t^8 * (t - 1)^8 * R21 (s21 t) (r21 t X)
      = q7 t ^ 4 * G21 t X := by
  field_simp [s21, r21, R21, q7, G21, ht0, ht1]
  ring

lemma quadDen_ne (r : ℚ) : r^2 - r + 1 ≠ 0 := by
  intro h
  have hs : 0 ≤ (2*r - 1)^2 := sq_nonneg (2*r - 1)
  nlinarith

lemma w_certificate (s r : ℚ) :
    (r^2 - r + 1)^2 *
        (w21 s r ^ 2 + (r + 1)*(3*r - 1)*(4*r + 1))
      = 4*r^3 * R21 s r := by
  field_simp [w21, quadDen_ne]
  ring

lemma map_to_E21 (s r : ℚ) (hR : R21 s r = 0) :
    OnE21 (e21x r) (e21y s r) := by
  have hw := w_certificate s r
  rw [hR, mul_zero] at hw
  field_simp [OnE21, e21x, e21y, w21, quadDen_ne] at *
  nlinarith
```

The final line may be more robust as `linear_combination` followed by `ring_nf`; the mathematical certificate is exactly

\[
 (r^2-r+1)^2(w^2+(r+1)(3r-1)(4r+1))=4r^3R_{21}.
\]

From `quotient_certificate`, nonvanishing of \(t^8(t-1)^8\) gives `R21 (s21 t) (r21 t X) = 0` whenever `G21 t X = 0`.

### 9.3 Point-exhaustion interface

Package the descent as

```lean
lemma E21_rat_points (x y : ℚ) (hE : OnE21 x y) :

    (x = -1/4  ∧ y = 1/8)   ∨
    (x = -1    ∧ y = -1)    ∨
    (x = -1    ∧ y = 2)     ∨
    (x = 5     ∧ y = 8)     ∨
    (x = 5     ∧ y = -13) := by
  -- explicit 2-isogeny/Kummer descent + 2-adic separatedness
  ...
```

The projective point \(O\) is handled separately.  Since `e21x r` is affine, the map never produces \(O\).

The descent proof should be split into the following reusable lemmas.

```lean
-- E₁ and E₂ affine equations
def OnE1 (u W : ℚ) : Prop := W^2 = u^3 + 25*u^2 + 144*u
def OnE2 (v V : ℚ) : Prop := V^2 = v^3 - 50*v^2 + 49*v

-- Explicit isogenies, with separate clauses for O and (0,0).
def phiX (u W : ℚ) : ℚ := W^2 / u^2
def phiY (u W : ℚ) : ℚ := W * (144 - u^2) / u^2

def dualX (v V : ℚ) : ℚ := V^2 / (4*v^2)
def dualY (v V : ℚ) : ℚ := V * (49 - v^2) / (8*v^2)
```

Prove by `field_simp; ring`:

* `phi_on_curve`;
* `dual_on_curve`;
* `dual_phi_eq_double`;
* `phi_dual_eq_double`.

For the Kummer kernels, use the explicit quadratic reconstructions displayed in §5.3 rather than invoking an abstract descent package.

### 9.8 Final theorem

```lean
theorem G21_ne_zero_of_t_ne_zero_one
    (t X : ℚ) (ht0 : t ≠ 0) (ht1 : t ≠ 1) :
    G21 t X ≠ 0 := by
  intro hG
  let s := s21 t
  let r := r21 t X
  have hR : R21 s r = 0 := by
    have hc := quotient_certificate t X ht0 ht1
    rw [hG, mul_zero] at hc
    have hfac : t^8 * (t - 1)^8 ≠ 0 := by
      exact mul_ne_zero (pow_ne_zero _ ht0) (pow_ne_zero _ (sub_ne_zero.mpr ht1))
    exact (mul_eq_zero.mp hc).resolve_left hfac

  let x := e21x r
  let y := e21y s r
  have hE : OnE21 x y := map_to_E21 s r hR
  have hcubic := s21_cubic t ht0 ht1

  rcases E21_rat_points x y hE with h | h | h | h | h | h | h
  · -- (-2,1): r=1/3, s=8, cubic has no rational root
    ...
  · -- (2,-1): r=-1, s=6
    ...
  · -- (-1/4,1/8): r=-1/4, s=-33/2
    ...
  · -- (-1,-1): r=0, s=0
    ...
  · -- (-1,2): impossible because r=0 forces w=-1, but this point has w=1
    ...
  · -- (5,8): r=-2, w=7, s=15/8
    ...
  · -- (5,-13): r=-2, w=-7, s=8
    ...

 theorem no_X1_21_nonCusp
    (t X Z : ℚ) (ht0 : t ≠ 0) (ht1 : t ≠ 1)
    (hG : G21 t X = 0) (hZ : Z^2 = D21 t X) : False := by
  exact (G21_ne_zero_of_t_ne_zero_one t X ht0 ht1) hG
```

The `hZ` hypothesis is intentionally unused.

## 10. What is and is not already in Mathlib

The quotient identities, the map to 21a1, the fiber calculations, and the rational-root exclusions are all routine `field_simp`, `ring`, `norm_num`, and the existing rational-root theorem.

The part that is not presently a one-line Mathlib import is the generic \(2\)-isogeny descent exactness plus formal-kernel separatedness.  The formulas and local obstruction certificates above reduce that infrastructure to a small, explicit development.  It should be proved as ordinary Lean theorems; it must not be inserted as an axiom or an opaque “rank-zero oracle.”

The cheapest formalization order is:

1. land the polynomial quotient/map certificates;
2. land the two explicit isogenies and their kernel reconstruction lemmas;
3. land the six mod-\(8/16\) Kummer obstruction lemmas;
4. prove the four-coset statement;
5. reuse or add the curve-specific \(2\)-adic separatedness lemma;
6. prove the eight-point exhaustion;
7. close `G21_ne_zero_of_t_ne_zero_one` by the seven finite cases.

That route is axiom-free and proves a strictly stronger result than the original \(G_{21}+D_{21}\) target.
